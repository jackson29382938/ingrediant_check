# Improvement audit — 2026-11-16

Reviewed **1.26.4**, latest remote `main` at **`893c713b553c2a48b91f02bf3d159407df6ecb80`**.
The workspace initially held 1.18.1; it was clean and was fast-forwarded before the reported checks.
The remote head was checked again during the audit and was unchanged. This is an audit: no runtime
code, scoring weights, deployment, account records or publication settings were changed.

The first improvements should be **cancelling queued AI work after opt-out**, **handling OCR
uncertainty in allergen words**, and **refusing unsegmented OCR as a complete label**. Automated
coverage is strong, but the real-photo checks found errors it does not catch. Several previously
unverified live flows now have evidence: retailer scores, Target sorting, extension-origin OCR,
backend product closure, token rejection and working USDA lookups.

Priorities: **P1** privacy or a missed selected allergen; **P2** material correctness/functionality;
**P3** verification and maintenance. A partial-reading warning or allergy disclaimer is a mitigation,
not evidence that the underlying transcription is accurate.

## Findings

### F1 — P1: Queued AI work sends after the feature is switched off and its key removed

**Location:** `src/worker/ai.js:109`, `:132`, `:202`, `:223`, `:245`.

`config()` checks the feature toggle, key and permission before adding work to the serial queue.
`complete()` later checks only `enabled`. The queued closure retains the old key. A request waiting
behind another request therefore sends even after `aiRatings=false` and `openrouterKey=''`.
The label-photo path uses the same queue and captured configuration.

**Reproduction:** hold the first mocked response, queue a second name, disable AI ratings and remove
the key, then release the first response. **Two requests** occur; the second sends `plimm` using
the removed key. See [ai-consent.json](audit-evidence-2026-11-16/ai-consent.json).

**Improve:** bind queued work to a configuration generation; recheck the relevant toggle, current
key and permission immediately before every fetch, including the structured-output retry. Cancel
queued work and abort applicable active requests on opt-out/key change. Keep explicit key testing
separate. Test ratings and photos, permission removal, key replacement and retry-after-opt-out.

### F2 — P1: High overall OCR confidence can hide a selected allergen

**Location:** `src/offscreen/ocr.js:61`, `src/engine/db/ocr-text.js:118`,
`src/content/product.js:563`, `src/options/library.js:128`.

On the real rice-noodle label for barcode **737628064502**, the visible text is
`HYDROLYZED SOY PROTEIN`; the shipped English OCR returns `HYDROLYZED SO¥ PROTEIN`, with
**91% overall confidence**. With soy, peanut and sesame warnings selected, the engine reports
peanut and sesame but **misses soy**. The actual upload UI reproduces this. The block remains
readable at 72% vocabulary coverage. Its unnamed-label score is **22**, versus **19** from the
manually transcribed ingredient list.

The panel already warns that absence of a warning is not an allergy safety check. The upload UI
also requires reviewing text and pressing Check. Those protections should stay; neither detects
this specific error. A global OCR-confidence threshold alone would not solve a 91% example.

**Improve:** retain word-level OCR confidence/uncertainty, highlight suspicious tokens near allergen
and charged-ingredient words in the review UI, and make uncertainty visible on automatic photo
readings. Ask for confirmation or another image rather than silently rewriting uncertain names.
Pin this photo and its manually reviewed allergen expectations as an OCR regression.

### F3 — P2: The upload UI scores raw OCR after ingredient-block extraction fails

**Location:** `src/options/library.js:130`, `:93`.

The upload path uses `ingredientsFromOcr(extracted) || extracted`. On the real French biscuit label
**7622210449283**, English OCR loses the ingredient heading, sugar, oils and several other words.
The block extractor correctly returns null, but the fallback feeds the entire garbled text to the
scorer. After pressing Check, the actual UI displays **65 / MEDIOCRE**, versus **27 / POOR** for
the manually transcribed visible ingredients. Recognition coverage is only **46%**.

The UI **does** display “Only 46% of this list was recognised” and “Partial reading”; this is not
an undisclosed complete reading. The improvement is to avoid a materially inflated numeric result
when the application cannot even establish an ingredient block. The automatic retailer pipeline
declines this particular no-heading result; the upload fallback is more permissive.

**Improve:** preserve raw OCR for editing, but require a validated block or explicit correction of
the ingredient list before assigning/saving a numeric result. Provide language and orientation
guidance. Do not add invented ingredient substitutions to make noisy output pass the gate.

### F4 — P2: OCR segmentation includes importer text and a second language's ingredient list

**Location:** `src/engine/db/ocr-text.js:85` (`BLOCK`), `:118` (`bestBlock`).

The rice-noodle block continues through `THIS PRODUCT CONTAINS PEANUTS`, `IMPORTED BY`, a street
address and a ZIP code. The actual bilingual Pringles photo **038000138416** continues through
`CONTAINS WHEAT INGREDIENTS` and a second `INGREDIENTES:` section. These are captured results,
not constructed phrasings. `BLOCK` stops at `CONTAINS:` but misses the no-colon form, the importer
heading, and the next ingredient heading. `bestBlock` prefers a longer block without checking
whether the extra text belongs to the ingredient list.

This contaminates entry counts, recognition coverage and possibly position-weighted penalties.
Pringles happens to remain **36** in this sample; do not present that one result as proof that
duplicated multilingual text is harmless. In the noodle example the full OCR path moves 19 to 22,
with transcription and segmentation errors both present.

**Improve:** segment ingredients, mandatory/advisory statements and manufacturer/importer text
separately; stop at a subsequent locale heading; preserve allergen statements in their own field.
Rank candidate blocks by validated completeness and quality rather than length alone. Check that
a first syntactically valid but unusable image does not prevent trying a later good image.

### F5 — P2: Scrolling a score into view immediately dismisses its new tooltip

**Location:** `src/options/sorted.js:478`, `:504`.

Both retailer-harness runs stop while hovering the second score in a long sorted list. A focused
real-extension probe records: `mouseenter` → tooltip added → `scroll` → tooltip removed, all in
about **3 ms**. The chip was below the viewport; scrolling it into view races its hover handler.
Leaving and entering again, or focusing it after scrolling, opens the tooltip successfully.

See [hover.json](audit-evidence-2026-11-16/hover.json). This is a real event-order issue, although
Playwright's scroll-into-view makes it particularly reproducible; it is not evidence that every
ordinary pointer hover fails.

**Improve:** settle/reposition hover after scrolling, or dismiss only a tooltip that was already
open before the scroll began. Keep Escape and keyboard focus behavior. Add an offscreen-chip
browser regression. Scrolling first in the harness can stabilize its later checks, but should not
be the only response to the underlying event-order problem.

### F6 — P2/P3: Validation tools and the current-validation document overstate coverage

**Locations:** `scripts/live-check.mjs:109`, `scripts/ui-review.mjs:63`,
`docs/CURRENT-VALIDATION.md:3`.

- `live-check` sets `ok` from HTTP/challenge/transport status alone. The sampled Costco product
  has **`ok:true` and `panel.state:"fail"`**, and Costco/Safeway searches have `ok:true` with zero
  numeric scores. Treat `ok` as transport accessibility, not extension success.
- `ui:check` fails because `getByLabel('Find a setting', {exact:true})` now matches both the new
  search landmark and its input. Use `getByRole('searchbox', {name:'Find a setting', exact:true})`.
  This is a test-locator regression, not proof the settings search is broken for users.
- `CURRENT-VALIDATION.md` still describes **1.19.2** and older retailer failures, and links to
  missing `docs/releases/1.19.2.md`. It conflicts with the current manifest and release history.

**Improve:** report transport, product identity, extraction, scoring, warnings and sorting as separate
outcomes; make expectations explicit per page type; retain blocked/unavailable as distinct states.
Update the current validation record with source commit, versions and links to dated evidence.

### F7 — P2: Database capture time hides how old a USDA source record is

**Location:** `src/worker/usda.js:65` (`parseSearch`).

The deployed USDA proxy successfully returned Cocoa Puffs barcode **016000275683**, with source
`modifiedDate:"2017-07-14"`, and Lifeway barcode **017077103329**, with date **2026-04-23**.
The client sorts records by source date but omits that date from the returned reading. A new local
capture consequently cannot explain whether the database label was maintained nine years ago or
this year. Exact barcode identity does not establish that a formula is current.

This is a freshness/provenance gap, not a finding that either retrieved ingredient list is false.
The existing “not from this page” database-source label is useful and should remain.

**Improve:** retain source modification/publication date separately from fetch time, show it with
database evidence, and flag older records for package comparison. Define a reviewed age policy
rather than assuming every newly downloaded record is a fresh label.

### F8 — P2 follow-up: USDA budget reservation is not atomic

**Location:** `docs/apps-script.gs:158`, `:210`.

Source inspection shows `CacheService.get(hour)` → compare → `put(used+1)` without a lock, while
USDA requests bypass the later write lock. Concurrent misses can reserve the same counter value;
cache eviction can also reset it within the hour. Thus 600/hour is a best-effort limit, not a hard
protection for the owner's 1,000/hour upstream quota.

**Improve:** reserve budget atomically under a short lock using a durable time-bucket counter,
then release it before the network request. Coalesce same-barcode misses where feasible. This was
**not stress-tested against production** and is not a claim that the live service exhausted quota.

### F9 — P2: The purchase-history table cannot receive keyboard focus for horizontal scrolling

**Location:** `src/options/history.html:45` (the table wrapper under the Entries section).

An independent axe-core 4.10.3 scan of the actual unpacked extension at 360 px flags
`scrollable-region-focusable` as **serious** in both light and dark themes. The failing node is
`section[aria-labelledby="entries"] > .table`. Its overflow area has neither a focusable wrapper
nor focusable content in the empty-history state tested. A keyboard reader cannot focus that area
to scroll its columns. This is a component scroll issue; the outer document itself does not overflow.

**Improve:** give the scroll wrapper `tabindex="0"`, an accessible name/association with the
Entries heading and a visible focus style. Verify arrow-key scrolling in empty and populated
tables at narrow widths. See [axe.json](audit-evidence-2026-11-16/axe.json). axe also marks several
checks incomplete for human review; a no-violation result is not a complete accessibility certification.

## Scope and remaining evidence

Manual tracing focused on photo acquisition/segmentation, score/warning acceptance, queued AI
consent and credential use, USDA transport/parsing, backend capabilities/authentication/closure,
retailer adapters and runner/sorting state, tooltip lifecycle, release workflows and validation
claims. The automated suite also covers personal-care/supplement classification, cached rescoring,
history deletion/account guards, message authorization, sanitization and hostile inputs.

Retailer public-page smoke tests do not establish signed-in carts, checkout, order history,
every SPA transition, regional inventory, every product format or all 21 Amazon storefronts.
Kroger-family React/hydration errors were captured; they are not attributed to the extension
without an extension-disabled comparison. No challenges were bypassed. Browser-store publication,
Google consent completion, independent health-model calibration, native-speaker localization
review and a live primary-source revalidation of supplement limits were not performed here.

Recommended order: F1–F2, then F3–F5, followed by truthful validation reporting and source freshness.
Keep source identity checks, local-only photo processing, explicit sharing consent, formula-safe
backend cells and the separation of warning flags from food scores intact.
