# Improvement audit — 2026-11-16

Reviewed `main` at **`893c713`**, extension **1.26.4**, the day after it was released. This is an
audit only: nothing in `src/` was edited. It looks where the audit of 2026-11-15 did not (unit
prices, localisation, storage, the website, claims and denials) and re-tests that audit's own fixes.
Priorities: **P1** = a number or warning the shopper sees is wrong; **P2** = a gap a user or an
attacker would notice; **P3** = follow-up.

The main results: the price per ounce is wrong by the pack size for several common ways of writing
a multi-pack, and the 1.26.4 parser fixes left four narrower formats unread. The German interface
is half translated.

Findings 1 to 7 are reproduced by `node docs/audit-verification-2026-11-16.cjs` (26 of 26 checks
reproduce on this commit; a fixed finding prints "fixed").

## P1 — a wrong number

### 1. The price per ounce ignores most pack multipliers

When Amazon shows its own unit price the extension uses it, and those were all read correctly.
Otherwise it works the price out from the title (`parseQuantity`, `src/engine/db/unit-price.js`),
and a pack is only recognised as "Pack of N", "N pack" or "N pk". Every other form is read as one
item, so the price per ounce comes out N times too high and a multi-pack sorts as the dearest item.

| Title | Holds | Read as |
|---|---|---|
| `Black Beans 15.5 oz (Case of 12)` | 186 oz | 15.5 oz |
| `Protein Bars 2.12 oz bars, box of 12` | 25.4 oz | 2.12 oz |
| `Sparkling Water 8 x 12 fl oz` | 96 fl oz | 12 fl oz |
| `Nudeln 500 g (5er Pack)` (how amazon.de writes every pack) | 2.5 kg | 500 g |
| `Pâtes 500 g (Lot de 6)`, `Pasta 500 g (Confezione da 6)`, `Arroz 1 kg (Paquete de 5)` | 6 or 5 packs | one |
| `Milk, 1/2 gallon` | 64 fl oz | 256 fl oz (the "2" of the fraction) |
| `Reis 1.000 g` on amazon.de | 1 kg | 1 g (the full stop groups thousands there) |

A weight beside a count ("1.4 oz, 12 Count") is declined, which is the safe answer and is left
alone here.

**Fix:** read "case of", "box of", "N boxes", "N x size" and "size x N", and each store's pack
wording ("Ner Pack", "Lot de N", "Paquete de N", "Confezione da N", "Verpakking van N"). Read a
fraction as a fraction or decline. Treat "." between digit groups as thousands on comma-decimal
stores. Where two readings disagree, decline, as the count rule already does.

### 2. An unnamed additive after a joining word

`Oats, salt, and preservative` scores 90; without "and" it scores 78. Seventy-two charged names
behave this way: "stabilizer", "color", "emulsifier" and their translations. The 1.26.4 fix skips a
function word after any leading word; that guard was meant only for "Contains: sweeteners".

**Fix:** apply the `isFunctionWord` guard in `withoutLeadWord` to the "contains:" form alone.

### 3. A purpose written after the ingredient

`Peaches, water, sugar added to promote color retention` scores 80 with no sugar charge, and
`Milk, salt, sugar added` scores 90 against 65. About 150 names whose rules are anchored at the end
of the entry (the sugars, mostly) are lost after "added", "added to …", "for …", "to …" and
"as …". Preservatives are unaffected: their rules are not anchored.

**Fix:** drop a trailing purpose clause when the rulebase then charges or reads more, the test the
other ambiguous readings use.

### 4. A footnote digit after a dye

`Oats, red 401, salt` (Red 40 with footnote 1) scores 90 against 18. The 1.26.4 rule drops a digit
only after a letter. This needs care: "E1422" must not become "E142", and "Red 33" (a cosmetic dye)
must not become "Red 3".

**Fix:** for a name, a space and a one- or two-digit number, drop one more trailing digit when that
charges more. Leave E-numbers and any number of four digits alone, and keep the food engine's
reading out of the personal-care path.

### 5. "Contains" before a capped ingredient is read as an allergen line

`Oats, salt. Contains soybean oil.` scores 94 and NOVA 1; the list with soybean oil scores 63.
`Oats, contains high fructose corn syrup, salt` scores 94 against 30. 1.26.4 fixed this for a list
headed "Contains:" and left a statement after or inside a list as it was.

**Fix:** an allergen line names sources, never a capped ingredient. Apply that test wherever a
declaration is recognised, not only at the head of the text.

### 6. A denial written as a sentence

`Oats, does not contain aspartame` is capped at 60 where `Oats, aspartame-free` scores 96.
`Contains no nuts.` and `Does not contain peanuts.` each raise the warning they deny, because
"contains no" passes as an allergen declaration.

**Fix:** add "does not contain" and "contains no" to the denial heads, and check for a denial
before reading a declaration.

### 7. Two formats 1.26.4 left

- A spaced en dash inside a comma list: `glucose – fructose syrup` scores 90 against 30. `score()`
  closes up a spaced keyboard hyphen there but not an en dash.
- Angle brackets round a word that starts with an accented letter: `<émulsifiant>` is left as
  written and unread. `ANGLE_TEXT` accepts only `a-z` first.

## P2 — gaps a user or an attacker would notice

### 8. The German interface is half translated

Preflight checks that every key has a German string. It cannot see text that has no key.

- `history.html`: all 153 pieces of text are hard-coded English, and `history.js` and
  `history-dashboard.js` never ask for a message.
- `options.html`: 117 of 307 pieces have no key. In a German-locale browser 62 visible passages are
  English, among them the goal descriptions and the allergy disclaimer.
- The reasons in the score panel ("Refined added sugar", "caps score at 30") come from the rule
  files and are English in every locale.
- One badge label in `src/content/search.js:255` is built in English.

The popup, setup, library and report pages are fully translated.

**Fix:** key the two pages, and add a check that fails on visible text without a key. Translating
the rule reasons (159 in the additives file alone) is a larger decision; saying in the store listing
that reasons are in English is the cheap alternative.

### 9. Keys and purchase history sit in storage every content script can read

`chrome.storage.local` is readable from content scripts unless the extension restricts it, and it
holds `openrouterKey`, `usdaKey`, the signed-in Google name and email, and up to 10,000 purchase
records. The content scripts never read them, but they run inside pages on sixteen stores; a flaw
in the browser's page process on any of them would expose all of it. `CLAUDE.md` says only the
worker reads the keys. That is a convention, not something the browser enforces.

**Fix:** move the two keys, the Google profile and the purchase history to IndexedDB in the
extension's origin (the worker already keeps the shared tables there, `worker/sheet-store.js`).
Content scripts cannot reach it, and nothing they need lives in those four items.

### 10. `docs/CURRENT-VALIDATION.md` describes 1.19.2

The README links it as the current validation record. It reports 1,261 tests for a release seven
versions back and links a release note that does not exist (`releases/1.19.2.md`).

**Fix:** regenerate it from the release run, or move it to `docs/history/` and link the release
notes instead.

## P3 — follow-up

### 11. Website: one table cannot be scrolled from the keyboard

On `stores.html` at phone width the sorting table's wrapper scrolls and cannot take focus. Add
`tabindex="0"` and a label, as the report page now has. Every other page, scheme and width is clean.

### 12. Vendored libraries are behind

DOMPurify is 3.4.16; 3.4.18 is current. tesseract.js is 5.1.1; 7.0.0 is current. Read the two
DOMPurify release notes and rebuild with `scripts/build-panel-sanitizer.mjs` if either is a
security fix. The OCR update is optional.

### 13. Four content scripts poll for a new address

`main.js`, `cart.js`, `orders.js` and `retailer.js` each compare `location.href` on a timer every
half second or second for as long as the tab is open. The Navigation API reports the same change
as an event.

### 14. Claims typed into the ingredient field are believed

Appending "organic, non-gmo, fermented, sprouted, live cultures" to a list of sugar and palm oil
raises it from 57 to 72. The bonus is bounded and labelled unverified, so this is noted, not
urgent. Counting a claim only when it qualifies an ingredient ("organic cane sugar") or is a
footnote would close it.

## What is in good order

- Every 1.26.4 parser fix reads the same in Spanish, German, French, Italian, Dutch, Swedish,
  Polish, Turkish and Portuguese.
- No personal-care or supplement rating changed in 1.26.4.
- Displayed unit prices are read correctly in dollars, cents, euros, pounds, rupees and yen.
- The privacy document matches what the code sends, to whom and by default.
- Badges, the panel and the hover card carry roles and labels, and honour reduced motion.
- Stuffing a title with claims adds at most 8 points, and denials in a list ("no sugar added",
  "sans sucre ajouté", "ohne Zuckerzusatz") are read as denials.
