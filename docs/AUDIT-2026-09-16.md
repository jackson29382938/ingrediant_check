# Rulebase, flags and gate audit — September 16, 2026

A full read of `src/`, the tests, the two earlier audits and the change set committed as
`7632930`, with every claim below reproduced by loading the engine in Node. The September 14 audit
was about what the rules and gates say on ordinary inputs; this one is about what the September 14
**fix** did to them, and about the two surfaces that audit left alone: the allergen and diet flags,
and the E-number vocabulary that fifteen of the twenty-one stores actually use.

**Resolution (same day).** Every item below was fixed in the commit that follows this one, and
each is pinned in `tests/audit-2026-09-16.test.js`, with eight new corpus entries covering the
label patterns the corpus had none of. The rulebase revision constant and its hash test
(`tests/rules-revision.test.js`) exist so item 3 cannot recur.

## Verdict

The engineering hygiene still holds, and the "Leave alone" list from September 14 is unchanged.
What this audit found was two **regressions introduced by that day's fix**, invisible to CI because
the corpus contained no label with a parenthetical function word and none with "natural flavors" —
the two most common features of a US ingredient panel — plus a rulebase that read EU stores'
additive names in four languages but not by the E-number those labels actually print.

## Regressions from `7632930`

1. **Parenthetical function words were charged as unnamed additives.** US labels write
   `Sodium Benzoate (Preservative)`, `Annatto (Color)`, `Xanthan Gum (Stabilizer)`. `expandEntry`
   promotes the parenthetical to its own entry, and the new `UNNAMED` rules are anchored to the
   whole entry, so the bare word fired. Measured: `Water, Sugar, Sodium Benzoate` 35 →
   `…(Preservative)` 28; `Cheese, Annatto` 90 → `Annatto (Color)` 81; `Emulsifier: Soy Lecithin`
   70 → `Emulsifier (Soy Lecithin)` 63. Disclosing more cost 7–9 points. Fixed by standing the
   unnamed charge down when anything at the same position is recognised by a named rule, a cap, or
   the panel vocabulary; a function word with nothing readable beside it is still charged.

2. **The `natural` qualifier double-charged every "natural flavor".** `QUALIFIERS` strips
   `natural`, so `natural flavors` was also tested as `flavors`, which hit the bare-flavouring
   rule in the same group (+3). `natural smoke flavor` was hit by the natural-flavour rule and the
   unanchored smoke rule as well (+9 over `smoke flavor`). Fixed: the stripped form may only add
   rules in groups the written form did not already hit, and the smoke rule no longer matches
   behind `natural`/`artificial`.

3. **Rule changes shipped with no cache invalidation.** `7632930` rewrote scoring in nine files
   under an unchanged `version: 1.0.1` (bumped in the previous commit), so every badge and popup
   card kept the pre-fix number for up to thirty days. Fixed structurally: `src/engine/db/revision.js`
   carries `RULES_REVISION`, the score cache fingerprints on it, and `tests/rules-revision.test.js`
   hashes the rule sources and fails until the constant and hash are both updated.

## Rulebase

4. **`mono- and diglycerides` never matched.** The rule allowed one optional separator on each side
   of "and", and the usual US spelling has a hyphen, a space, "and", a space. Every sandwich loaf
   with the most common emulsifier in the aisle was uncharged. Also now matched: E471, E472a–f,
   `monoglycerides`, `diglycerides`.

5. **EU labels name additives by E-number, and the rulebase knew sixteen.** `sweeteners (E951,
   E950)` scored 65 Mediocre against 22 Avoid for `aspartame, acesulfame K`, and was shown rather
   than declined (coverage 40%). The Southampton six azo colours — which carry a mandatory EU
   warning — were absent under four of their six numbers and all of their names. Every rule with a
   code now carries it (`\be[- ]?NNN\b`, accepting "E322", "e-322", "E 322"); E104/E122/E124/E131/
   E142/E151/E155 have their own caps; E322 is charged as lecithin of unstated source; benign codes
   and natural colours (annatto, carotene, beet, carmine) are recognised so an EU list written in
   codes clears the coverage gate. Also broadened: `sodium phosphate` and `calcium phosphate` as
   phosphate additives, `tocopherols`, `silica`, `benzoic acid`, `potassium nitrite`.

6. **Cane sugar was charged twice.** The first REFINED rule accepted `cane sugar` and the cane rule
   accepted it too; `Cane sugar` alone cost 21 against 17 for `Sugar`. And with only the cane row
   left, `Organic cane sugar` scored 81 Good on the organic bonus. A label claim lifts a product,
   not an ingredient, so bonuses are withheld when every ingredient is itself penalised or capped:
   organic cane sugar is 73, the same as sugar.

## Flags

7. **`malt\w*` flagged maltodextrin, maltitol and maltose as wheat.** Maltodextrin is in most of
   the processed aisle, so a coeliac user saw "Wheat / gluten" on gluten-free products constantly.
   Sweet potato was a nightshade (it is a morning glory), a Cornish hen was corn, and rice bran oil
   was not keto.

8. **Vegan and vegetarian missed most of the animals labels name.** The vegan pattern had the
   literal word `shellfish`; shrimp, prawn, crab, duck, ham, bacon, sausage, veal, venison, albumen,
   isinglass, beeswax and E120/E441 all passed. Both diets now share explicit meat and sea lists,
   with guards for coconut meat, vegetable broth, hamburger, gooseberry, crabapple and microbial
   rennet.

9. **The flags read English only, on fifteen non-English stores.** Milk, egg, gluten grains, soy,
   peanut, tree nuts, fish, crustaceans and molluscs, sesame and sulphites are now read in Spanish,
   French, German and Italian, with plant-milk exclusions in each ("leche de coco", "lait d'amande",
   "Kokosmilch", "latte di mandorla") and nutmeg excluded from tree nuts in each. Celery, mustard
   and lupin — on the EU's mandatory list and not the US one — are new flags. `\b` does not see an
   accented letter as part of a word, so "blé", "œuf", "maní" and "épeautre" carry explicit
   boundaries.

## The food gate

10. **Real groceries got the "not food" card from the title alone.** `Funnel Cake Mix` (`funnel`),
    `Halo Top Protein Ice Cream` and `Coconut Oil Cream Cheese` (`protein … cream`), `Ketchup with
    Flip Cap` (`cap`), `Sparkling Water, 24 Count Case` (`case`). `cap`, `hat` and `watch` now need
    an apparel word beside them; `cream` is not topical when it is ice cream, cream cheese, sour
    cream or a creamer; `funnel cake` and `N count case` are groceries. Bar soap is now a cosmetic.

## Smaller

- The paste box focused itself on mount, on every product page Amazon publishes no ingredient text
  for, and redirected whatever the person was typing into Amazon's search box. It no longer does.
- Hover and auto-score armed `[data-asin]` carousels on product pages, fetching a product page on
  hover only to find no tile heading to paint. Both now arm on results pages only.
- `panel.js` re-listed the refined-sugar rule names to decide whether to hide the "natural sugar
  not counted" note, and had drifted from `sugars.js` once already. Penalty rows now carry `cat`
  and a `refined` mark from the engine.
- The corpus gains eight entries: a natural-flavour soda, a ranch dressing with every function word
  disclosed in parentheses, annatto cheddar, a German chocolate and a Spanish diet cola written in
  E-numbers, EU azo-coloured gummies, a bag of organic cane sugar, and a sandwich loaf with
  `mono- and diglycerides`.

## Not done, and why

- **`.chrome-profile/` is still in the checkout** (11 MB; `Cookies`, `Login Data`, `History`, last
  written September 13). It is git-ignored but the folder syncs to Drive. The September 14 fix moved
  the *default*; the old directory holds logins, so deleting it is a decision for the owner:
  `rm -rf .chrome-profile`.
- **`web_accessible_resources` still exposes `vendor/tesseract/*`** to Amazon pages. The offscreen
  document is extension-origin and should not need it, but the September 14 audit asked for a live
  OCR run before removing it, and this session had no live browser with the extension loaded.
- `_locales/` still has only `en`, and the settings page is hard-coded English. Unchanged.

## Leave alone

Everything on the September 14 list, plus the qualifier strip itself (it still catches "organic
agave"), the position-sharing of parenthetical sub-ingredients (it is what makes item 1 findable),
and the coverage gate's threshold, which the E-number work respected rather than lowered.
