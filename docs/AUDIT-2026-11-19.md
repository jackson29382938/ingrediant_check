# Improvement audit — 2026-11-19

Reviewed `main` at **`893c713`**, extension **1.26.4**, four days after the 2026-11-15 audit was carried out. This is
an audit, not an implementation change: nothing in `src/` was edited. Priorities give the recommended order of
work: **P1** = a score or warning the shopper sees is wrong; **P2** = the page freezes or the release is at risk;
**P3** = follow-up improvement.

Every automated check passes, the accessibility fixes from 1.26.4 hold, and all 30 checks of the previous audit
still print "fixed". The parser now reads almost every way a list is *written*. What it does not yet know is
many of the *names* an ingredient is sold under. A sugar, starch, oil or dye under its second name, its British
spelling or its Indian wording is charged nothing, and so is a processed food whose name ends in a whole-food
noun ("mechanically separated chicken", "sweetened condensed milk"). Those are the first things to fix.
The previous audit probed formats; this one probed names, which is why it finds what that one could not.

Every finding is reproduced by `node docs/audit-verification-2026-11-19.cjs` (87 of 87 checks reproduce on this
commit; a fixed finding prints "fixed"; `--quick` skips the three timed checks, about 20 seconds).

## P1 — a score the shopper sees is wrong

`CLAUDE.md`: nothing is charged that no rule matches, so silence reads as cleanliness. In every case below the list
still reads as readable, and in most of them the entry is also counted as *read*, so the coverage line stays at
100% and the panel gives no hint.

Each fix moves scores, so each needs a `RULES_REVISION` bump, a regenerated scoring inventory, the names added to a
test, and a release note.

### 1. A processed animal or dairy food is read as the whole food

`labels.WHOLE` (`src/engine/db/labels.js:71`, `:72`, `:74`) accepts any 24 to 28 characters in front of a meat, fish or
dairy noun. It guards the words that come *after* the noun (`DERIVED`: "chicken flavor", "milk chocolate") and the
plant words *before* it (`NOT_DAIRY`: "oat milk"), but not processing words before it. `sugars.FREE`
(`src/engine/db/sugars.js:16`) has the same open prefix for milk and cream, so the sugar in a sweetened dairy word
costs nothing either.

| Entry | Read as | Should be |
|---|---|---|
| sweetened condensed milk, chocolate milk, sugar milk | NOVA 1, no charge | milk plus added sugar |
| ice cream, frozen yogurt, sweetened whipped cream, yogurt coating | NOVA 1, no charge | sweetened, often NOVA 4 |
| mechanically separated chicken / turkey, chicken nuggets, chicken hot dog, breaded chicken | NOVA 1 | NOVA 4 or at least processed |
| processed cheese, pasteurized process cheese food, cheese product, imitation cheese, vegan cheese | NOVA 1 | NOVA 4 |
| non-dairy cream, imitation shrimp | NOVA 1 | not a whole food at all |

`Sweetened condensed milk, coffee` scores **90**; `Milk, sugar, coffee` scores **65**.

**Fix:** in both files, replace the open prefix for animal and dairy nouns with the closed list of minimal-processing
words the plain-milk line (`labels.js:70`) already uses (pasteurized, homogenized, organic, whole, skim, grass-fed,
free-range, boneless, skinless, ground…), or add a look-behind for processing words: `sweetened|condensed|chocolate|
flavou?red|malted|ice|frozen|whipped|non-dairy|imitation|vegan|plant-based|processed|process|mechanically separated|
breaded|fried|coated`. Extend `DERIVED` with `nuggets|hot dogs?|patties|product|food|coating|topping|spread`. Charge
"sweetened condensed milk" and "condensed milk" (sweetened in both US and UK usage) as a refined sugar. Add the rows
above to `tests/invariants.test.js` with their expected NOVA class.

### 2. Sugars and sweeteners the rulebase does not name

The sugar rules are anchored to whole entries (`^…$`), so a name is charged only if it is spelled exactly as written
in `sugars.js`. "Muscovado sugar" is charged; "muscovado" is not.

**2a — not read at all** (charged nothing; coverage drops a little):

| Written | Scores | Same thing, as the rules know it |
|---|---|---|
| muscovado, jaggery, panela, rapadura, sucanat, turbinado, Florida crystals | 90 | sugar: 65 |
| isoglucose (the EU's regulatory name for glucose-fructose syrup) | 90 | glucose-fructose syrup: **30** |
| dextrose monohydrate, anhydrous dextrose | 90 | dextrose: 65 |
| fruit sugar | 90 | fructose: 63 |
| trehalose, isomaltulose, tagatose, isomalto-oligosaccharide | 90 | sugar: 65 |
| acesulphame K (a common UK and Indian spelling; `sugars.js:93` has no `-sulph-`) | 90 | acesulfame K: 60 |
| rebaudioside A / M, luo han guo, mogrosides, thaumatin, neohesperidin DC (E957, E959) | 90 | reb A: 51, monk fruit: 53 |
| dextrin, resistant dextrin | 90 | maltodextrin: 41 |

**2b — counted as read, charged nothing.** `COMMON_PANEL` (`src/engine/db/vocabulary.js`) holds the bare words
`syrup` (line 56), `juice` and `extract` (28), `malt` and `maize` (60), `flour` and `starch` (45). They make any
unknown syrup or juice count as read, so the coverage line says 100%, the panel is silent, and **AI help never sees
the entry** (`toAsk` is given `result.unrecognised` only, `src/content/product.js:73`):

| Written | Scores | Same thing, as the rules know it |
|---|---|---|
| fructose syrup, dextrose syrup, sugar syrup, invert syrup, cane invert syrup, beet syrup | 90 | fructose 63, dextrose 65, invert sugar 64 |
| glucose syrup solids | 90 | glucose syrup 65; corn syrup solids **30** |
| malt syrup, rice malt syrup, oat syrup, cassava syrup, chicory root syrup, coconut syrup, yacon syrup | 90 | barley malt syrup 67, rice syrup 65 |
| palm sugar, coconut palm sugar, grape sugar, corn sweetener | 90 | coconut sugar 84 |
| dehydrated cane juice, cane juice crystals | 90 | evaporated cane juice 65 |
| white grape juice concentrate, pear juice concentrate, concentrated apple juice | 90 | apple juice concentrate 81 |

This is exactly how a label hides sugar. The same cereal with its sugar split four ways scores **37** under names the
rules know and **65** under names they do not, better than one honest "Sugar" listed first (63).

**Fix:**
- Add the names above to `sugars.js`, with the bare forms of the qualified ones already there (muscovado, turbinado,
  demerara, caster, icing without "sugar").
- Make the dextrose, fructose and glucose rules accept `syrup`, `solids`, `monohydrate` and `anhydrous`.
- Add `-sulph-` to the acesulfame rule, the full stevia and monk-fruit names, and E957 and E959.
- Make the fruit-juice-concentrate rule read any fruit before `juice concentrate` and "concentrated … juice".
- Then narrow `COMMON_PANEL`: a bare `syrup` should not make an entry read while no sugar rule charges it. Keep
  `maple syrup` and the named ones, and let an unknown syrup count as unread so coverage and AI help can see it.

### 3. The same additive under its second name

| Written | Scores | Same thing, as the rules know it |
|---|---|---|
| food starch-modified (the name in 21 CFR 172.892, printed on US labels) | 90 | modified food starch: 45 |
| acetylated distarch adipate, hydroxypropyl distarch phosphate, acetylated distarch phosphate, starch sodium octenyl succinate, oxidized starch | 90 | E1422 / E1442: 45 |
| soya oil, maize oil (UK and India), sunflower seed oil | 90 | soybean / corn / sunflower oil: capped at **63** (`oils.js:19`, `:20`, `:22`) |
| artificial sweetener, artificial sweeteners | 90 | "sweetener": 51 |
| red dye 40, red dye #40, yellow dye 5 | 90 | red 40: **18** |
| sodium acid pyrophosphate, tetrasodium pyrophosphate, sodium hexametaphosphate | 90 | disodium phosphate 70, sodium tripolyphosphate 47 |
| cellulose powder, cellulose | 90 | powdered cellulose: 68 |
| textured wheat protein | 90 | textured soy protein: 38 |
| all-purpose flour, plain flour, bread flour, cake flour, self-raising flour | 90 | wheat flour 67, white flour 45 |
| sucrose esters of fatty acids (E473), dimethylpolysiloxane (E900), shellac / confectioner's glaze (E904), sodium erythorbate (E316), dimethyl dicarbonate, lauric arginate | 90 | an emulsifier or preservative is charged |

Their E-numbers (E473, E900, E904, E316) are not read either.

**Fix:** add each alternate name to the rule that already charges the canonical one; add `soya`, `maize` and
`sunflower seed` to the three oil rules; treat "dye" between a colour and its number as the colour; read "food

Decide whether "all-purpose" and "plain" flour should cost what "white flour" costs; today they cost nothing,
less than "wheat flour".

### 4. Indian labels (amazon.in)

amazon.in is a supported store, and Indian labels follow FSSAI conventions the parser does not read:

| Written | Scores | Plain |
|---|---|---|
| `colour (INS 102)`, `colour (INS102)` | 49 | tartrazine: **18** |
| `colour (INS 150d)` | 58 | caramel colour: 30 |
| `permitted synthetic food colours` (the standard FSSAI phrase for an artificial colour) | 54 | artificial colour: 20 |
| `liquid glucose` | 65 | glucose syrup: 54 |

`E_CODE` (`score.js:552`) reads `102` and `E102` in a function word's bracket, but not `INS 102`, which Indian (and
Codex) labels use. Australian bare numbers (`preservative (211)`) and UK E-numbers already work.

**Fix:** accept `INS\s?` wherever an `E` prefix is accepted; charge "permitted synthetic … colour(s)" as an artificial
colour; add "liquid glucose" to the glucose rule. Add one real Indian label to `tests/corpus.json`.

### 5. "Percent" written as a word

`MINOR_BLOCK` (`score.js:882`) reads `2%` but not `2 percent` or `two percent`. The block marker is then not
recognised, the words stay glued to the next ingredient, and that ingredient is charged nothing:

| Written | Scores | Plain |
|---|---|---|
| `Oats, contains 2 percent or less of sugar, salt` | 90 | 65 |
| `Oats, contains less than 2 percent of sugar, salt` | 90 | 65 |

**Fix:** accept `\s?(?:%|per ?cent)` and the number words one to five in `MINOR_BLOCK` and `LEADING_SHARE`
(`TRAILING_SHARE` already reads `per ?cent`), and add the phrasing to the minor-block test in `tests/invariants.test.js`.

## P1 — allergen and diet warnings

### 6. Compound foods whose allergen is not in the name; plant-based names that raise a dairy warning

Flags never move a score, but `allergens.js` is hashed, so the revision still needs a bump. The popup and panel
already say the flags are not allergy advice. On a US label the separate "Contains:" line would raise most of
these, which limits the harm; EU and Indian labels mark allergens in bold, which does not survive as text.

| Allergen | Not flagged |
|---|---|
| Wheat / gluten | soy sauce, teriyaki sauce (soy sauce is brewed with wheat; tamari often is not), beer, ale, brewer's yeast, roux |
| Sesame | hummus (tahini), furikake, dukkah |
| Milk | white chocolate, dahi, malai, khoya (Indian dairy) |
| Egg | mayo, hollandaise, custard |
| Fish | imitation crab (surimi is already flagged), caesar dressing |
| Peanut | satay sauce |
| Soy | gochujang, doenjang, hoisin sauce |
| Sulfites | wine, wine vinegar, dried apricots |

False alarms, each in front of a shopper who chose that filter:

| Written | Raises |
|---|---|
| vegan cheese, dairy-free cheese, plant-based cheese, vegan butter | Milk **and** Not vegan |
| plant-based milk | Milk and Not vegan |
| egg-free mayonnaise | Egg |

`detect` takes the "-free" claim out of the entry (finding 7 of the last audit) and then reads the noun the claim was

**Fix:** add the words to `db/allergens.js` and the pairs to `tests/synonyms.test.js`; treat `vegan|plant-based|
dairy-free|egg-free` + the food they qualify as one claim in `withoutFreeClaims`.

## P2 — page responsiveness

### 7. A long run of words broken at a hyphen freezes the tab for seconds

`rejoinWrapped` (`score.js:765`) tries both readings of every `word-␣word` in an entry and, for each one, recomputes
`chargeOf` and `recognised` over the **whole entry**: every rule, every time. The lookup memo is skipped above 120
characters. So the cost is (hyphens × entry length × rules), which is quadratic in the entry length:

| Input inside the 6,000-character extractor limit (`extract.js:33`) | Time |
|---|---|
| `"ab- "` repeated | **11.3 s** |
| `"partially hydro- genated "` repeated | 5.6 s |
| `"aaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaa-\n"` repeated | 2.0 s |
| the same at 60,000 characters (more than any input path allows; shows the growth) | 207 s |

Every input path caps a list at 6,000 characters (`extract.js`, the retailer adapters, the paste box in
`library.html`), so the first three rows are the realistic worst case. The engine runs in the content script, on
the store page's own thread, so the tab stops responding for that long.
No real label has thousands of line-end hyphens; a seller's text field or a garbled OCR result can.
`tests/hostile-input.test.js` sets a 400 ms limit and would catch this, but none of its pieces has whitespace after
the hyphen (`'a-'`, `'ab-cd '`), so the regex never matches.

The CPU profile puts 2.15 s of 2.2 s (at 6,000 characters) inside `rejoinWrapped`.

**Fix:** stop trying after a fixed number of wraps per entry (a real ingredient has one or two), or skip entries
longer than a few hundred characters (`LONG_ENTRY` is 80); compute `chargeOf(out)` once per accepted change instead
of once per candidate. Add `'ab- '` and `'hydro- genated '` to `PIECES` in `tests/hostile-input.test.js`.

## P3 — follow-up

### 8. Characters that still hide an ingredient

| Written | Scores | Plain |
|---|---|---|
| `high−fructose corn syrup` with U+2212 (minus sign), as some PDF exports print a hyphen | 90 | 30 |
| `Oats—sugar—salt`: em dashes with no spaces as separators | 90 | 65 |
| `сane sugar` with a Cyrillic `с` (378 of 449 charged names lose their charge to one look-alike letter) | 90 | 65 |

The first is a one-character addition to `TYPED_HYPHEN` (`score.js:1041`). The second is rare. The third is not a
real-label problem but a cheap trick for a seller who wants a better score: fold Cyrillic and Greek look-alikes to
Latin in `cleanText`, but only inside a word that also has Latin letters, so a real Cyrillic list stays as it is.

### 9. The shopper cannot see what was not read

- The panel shows "N% of the list recognised" when coverage is below 100% (`panel.js:308`), but never names the
  entries. A list with "muscovado" in it says "90% recognised", and nobody can tell which tenth. Name them
  ("Not recognised: muscovado") in the footer.
- `COMMON_PANEL`'s generic words (`syrup`, `juice`, `malt`, `flour`, `starch`, `extract`) let an unknown sweetener or
  starch count as read. Coverage then reports 100%, and the optional AI help, which only rates unread entries, never
  sees them. Finding 2b is the result. A word whose head noun is a sugar, sweetener, starch or oil should count as
  read only when a rule charges it or a free list (`sugars.FREE`, `labels.WHOLE`) accepts it.
- A **name-equivalence test** would have caught findings 2 to 4: a table in which every row is a canonical name and
  its alternates (spellings, E/INS numbers, legal names, "solids"/"syrup"/"monohydrate" forms) and the test asserts
  they charge the same, as `tests/synonyms.test.js` does for allergens. The verification script's rows are a start.

### 10. Small things

- The settings page's jump chips (Detail · Goals · Warnings · Avoid · Rate…) scroll sideways at 360 px; the clipped
  last chip is the only cue that there are more.
- `glucose syrup solids` is charged nothing while `corn syrup solids` is capped at 30; whichever finding 2 decides,
  the two should agree.
- `palm kernel oil`, `palm olein`, `palm stearin` and `fractionated palm kernel oil` are counted as read and charged
  nothing, as plain palm oil is by design (`oils.TRADITIONAL`). Fractionated and kernel oils are refined industrial
  fats; worth a deliberate decision.

## What is in good order

- All 1,423 tests, coverage floors, lint, preflight, both packages, the whole Chromium harness, the retailer harness,
  the website check and the Firefox lint pass on this commit.
- The 1.26.4 accessibility work holds: axe-core reports nothing on any of the eight pages in either scheme.
- Every one of the 93 hosts the manifest injects into passes the worker's sender check, and no Amazon content host is
  missing from `host_permissions`. No `web_accessible_resources`, no `externally_connectable`, and no message
  listener on `window`.
- The four `innerHTML` assignments in `src/` are constant strings; fetched store pages are parsed with `DOMParser`,
  which runs no script.
- The AI path takes only a class id from a closed list, for a name that was asked, under a strict JSON schema, and
  keeps its pause in session storage.
- Storage is bounded (10,000 product entries, time limits, capped AI caches), as `unlimitedStorage` requires.
- The parser now reads every format the last audit listed, and many more: UK E-numbers, Australian bare numbers,
  Canadian bilingual lists, ALL CAPS, semicolons, CRLF, unbalanced brackets and "to preserve freshness" brackets
  all score as the plain list does.
- No innocent food was charged by a word that looks like an additive (sugar snap peas, hearts of palm, butter
  beans, cream of tartar, sweet corn, honeydew).
- Splitting sugar across names the rules know is penalised, not rewarded (37 against 63 for one sugar first).
