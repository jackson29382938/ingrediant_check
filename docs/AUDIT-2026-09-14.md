# Rulebase and gate audit — September 14, 2026

A full read of `src/`, the tests, and the change set committed as `84faf9d`, with every claim
below reproduced by loading the engine in Node. Nothing in this document has been fixed yet; it is
the work list. The two earlier audits covered worker reliability and the installed extension; this
one is about what the rules and gates actually say on ordinary inputs.

## Verdict

The engineering hygiene holds up: every panel interpolation is escaped, storage is sanitized on
read, the worker's message handlers and queue serialization are correct, and the panel arithmetic
reconciles. The remaining risk sits in the **rulebase and the gates** — anchored or unanchored
regexes that produce confident wrong answers on common inputs, and two gate heuristics that refuse
or stand down on ordinary grocery listings. Every item in "Do first" is a small change.

## Do first

1. **"Organic" launders almost every sweetener.** Most sugar rules are anchored `^…$` with no
   optional qualifier, so `organic agave nectar` matches nothing while `agave nectar` costs 16 points,
   and the organic bonus still pays out. Measured: `Oats, agave nectar` → 63 Mediocre;
   `Oats, organic agave nectar` → 95 Excellent. Same for brown rice syrup, tapioca syrup, coconut
   sugar, dextrose, glucose syrup, erythritol (47 → 95) and fruit juice concentrate. Only cane sugar,
   honey and maple handle the prefix. Strip a leading qualifier run
   (`^(?:organic|raw|pure|natural|non-gmo|certified|blue)\s+`) before testing anchored rules in
   `score.js` (the `PENALTIES` loop), or unanchor the heads in `sugars.js`. Pin each pair as a
   regression test.

2. **"Case of 24" is judged non-food.** `supplementEquipment` in `ingestible.js` counts bare
   `case` and `scoop` as −8 with nothing to offset them. On the product page `quickNonFood()` in
   `main.js` shows *Not scored — this does not look like food* for "LaCroix Sparkling Water (Case of
   24)", "Blue Diamond Almonds, Case of 6" and "Whey Protein with Scoop". On search, those tiles are
   skipped, and for a department-less search such as `k=sparkling water` six of eight realistic
   titles read non-food, so `looksNonFoodFromResults` stands the whole page down. Use
   `\bcase\b(?!\s+of\s+\d)` and drop `scoop`, or keep `supplementEquipment` out of the title-only
   path.

3. **Real lists are rejected for a trailing boilerplate sentence.** `looksLikeIngredients` splits
   only on `,;`, so "Peanuts, Salt. Made in a facility that processes tree nuts." is two parts, one
   matching `NON_INGREDIENT` → 50% ≥ 0.34 → rejected. Also rejected: "Organic rolled oats. Product
   of Canada." and "Almonds, sea salt. Contains: tree nuts. Packed in the USA." The user gets the
   paste box for a published list. Split off sentences (`\.\s+(?=[A-Z])`) and drop the ones matching
   `NON_INGREDIENT` or `^(contains|may contain)` before the density test.

4. **Allergen and diet flags fire on non-dairy "butter" and "milk".** `cocoa butter`,
   `shea butter`, `peanut butter`, `coconut milk`, `almond milk` and `cream of tartar` all flag Milk,
   Not vegan and Not dairy-free; `almond flour` and `coconut flour` flag Not keto; `corned beef`
   flags Corn. `score.js` already carries `NOT_REAL_BUTTER` for exactly this; apply the same
   exclusions in `allergens.js` and add plant-milk lookbehinds. Conservative is the stated policy,
   but "cocoa butter contains milk" is not a borderline call.

5. **The housekeeping alarm is probably never firing.** `background.js` calls
   `chrome.alarms.create('air-housekeeping', …)` at top level, so it runs on every worker start.
   Creating an alarm with an existing name replaces and re-times it; with no `delayInMinutes` the
   first fire is twelve hours out, and a worker that wakes for every product page keeps pushing it.
   `sweepCache` then runs only on browser restart, and the twice-daily diagnostics flush promised in
   DIAGNOSTICS.md is dead — batches under twelve rows sit until the next scored product. Guard with
   `chrome.alarms.get(...)` or create in `onInstalled`. To confirm: `chrome.alarms.getAll()` in the
   worker console before and after a worker restart; if `scheduledTime` moves, this is real.

6. **The score cache stores up to 12 KB of ingredient text per entry that nothing reads.**
   `cacheScore` in `main.js` writes it; the only reader is the non-empty check in
   `score-cache.js`. With `MAX_ENTRIES = 5000` and no `unlimitedStorage`, the theoretical footprint
   is six times the 10 MB quota, and quota failures are swallowed. Drop the field, or cap it near
   1,500 characters and lower the entry cap. Related: the fingerprint keys on `sheetIndex.t`, so every
   six-hour sheet refresh retires the entire score cache, and every content script loads the full
   sheet index at boot just to read `.t`. Store a small revision key instead.

7. **Whole-food detection is unanchored at the tail.** `labels.WHOLE` matches anything that
   *starts* with `milk|cream|butter|chicken|beef…`, so a lone `Milk chocolate` → 96 Excellent NOVA 1,
   `Butter flavored oil` → 97, `Chicken flavor` → 86 NOVA 1. Amazon's field does sometimes hold just
   "Milk Chocolate". Anchor with `$` plus a lookahead against
   `chocolate|flavou?r|powder|solids|substitute|stock|broth`.

8. **The offscreen OCR document is never closed.** `closeDocument` appears nowhere in `src/`;
   once OCR runs, the Tesseract worker stays resident until the browser restarts. Close the document
   from the worker after about two minutes idle; `ensureOffscreen` already recreates it on demand and
   the cancellation plumbing in `ocr.js` makes the idle timer straightforward.

9. **Declare `minimum_chrome_version`.** The worker depends on `AbortSignal.any` and
   `runtime.getContexts`, both Chrome 116+. Without the manifest field, older Chrome installs the
   extension and every network path throws with no user-visible reason. Add
   `"minimum_chrome_version": "116"`.

10. **The development Chrome profile lives inside a Google-Drive-synced folder.** `scripts/ab.mjs`
    defaults the profile to `<repo>/.chrome-profile`, which holds `Cookies`, `Login Data` and
    `History` and is synced to Drive even though git ignores it. Default to `os.tmpdir()` or
    `~/.cache/ingredient-check/profile`.

11. **Calibration to decide on, not a bug.** `wheat flour` is 12 points and `byPts(12)` makes it a
    hard ultra-processing marker, so `Whole wheat flour, water, salt, yeast` → 42 Poor, NOVA 4, while
    a bag of `Sugar` → 77 Good with a "Single-ingredient whole food" bonus. Both contradict the NOVA
    framing the panel shows and the "Refined grains" help text in `categories.js`. If gluten aversion
    is the intent, say so in the label; otherwise make plain flour `soft` and keep sugar and oils out
    of the simplicity bonus.

12. **Non-English stores score higher than their English twins.** The non-English staples in
    `COMMON_PANEL` let ES/FR/DE lists clear the coverage gate, but the penalty rules stay English:
    the Spanish twin of a chocolate list scores 63 against 57 because `aroma` and `lecitina de soja`
    match nothing. Either add synonyms for the twenty or so highest-frequency additives per supported
    language, or raise the coverage threshold on non-English hosts so those lists are refused rather
    than flattered.

## Then

- **Engine.** `splitTopLevel` splits on `.`, so "contains 2.5% or less of" (not caught by the `\d+%`
  collapse) yields junk entries `contains 2` / `: salt` that shift positions and lower coverage. The
  CAP dedup in `score.js` compares against a string that always carries a suffix, so it never
  matches — `high oleic sunflower oil` and `partially hydrogenated soybean oil` render two or three
  CAP rows for one ingredient.
- **Extraction.** Every nutrition regex uses `[\d.]+`, so EU decimal commas (`Zucker 12,5 g`) never
  parse and the nutrition categories are inert on the fifteen non-US stores. The `addedSugar`
  pattern can read the next row's number on an FDA panel ("Includes 10g Added Sugars / Protein 3g"
  → 3).
- **Security and privacy.** `web_accessible_resources` exposes `vendor/tesseract/*` and
  `src/offscreen/*` to Amazon pages, but the offscreen document and its worker are extension-origin
  and should not need it. Verify in a live session, then remove and update `preflight`; it is a
  fingerprinting surface for no benefit.
- **Localisation and duplication.** Hard-coded English remains in `panel.js` ("means nothing
  here") and `popup.js` (save and permission messages, source labels). `NOVA_SHORT` and `bandColor`
  in `popup.js` duplicate `NOVA_TEXT` / `BAND_COLOR` in `panel.js` and `badgeColor` in
  `background.js`; one small shared module would end the drift.
- **Tests.** The corpus has no `organic <sweetener>`, no trailing-boilerplate list, no "Case of N"
  title and no compound single entry — each item above should land as a regression case when it is
  fixed. CI runs Node 20 only; add 22.

## Leave alone

Panel rendering (every interpolation goes through `esc`), the prefs sanitizers and URL validators,
denial and negation handling, penalty saturation and the shave reconciliation, the serialized
diagnostics queue and its acknowledge-before-delete rule, the `return true` discipline in the
message handlers, and the cache-fingerprint and OCR-cancellation designs landed in `84faf9d`. All
checked; all sound.
