`npm run preflight`, `npm run lint` and `npm run harness:ci` before it was pushed; the full workflow
also passed on GitHub's runners (Actions → *test* → *Run workflow*). `git log` has the detail; this

## Behaviour a user will notice

- **Sharing product readings with other computers is off by default** (new setting,
  `sharedProducts`), and the receiver neither stores nor serves them until opened. Search results are
  therefore read from Amazon more often. The certification tables stay on ([CACHE.md](../CACHE.md),
  *Trust*).
- **Sorting** appears only on amazon.com, .co.uk, .ie, .ca, .com.au, .sg and .in, with prices in the
  store's currency and per 100 g on metric stores; the README calls it a preview.
- **More allergen and diet warnings**: the declaration words themselves ("tree nuts", "shellfish",
  "nuts", and their Spanish, Italian, French and German forms), allergens named only by the food
  (parmesan, panko, surimi, pesto, halva…), and meat words in every label language. Milk
  thistle, crab apple, bean curd and similar no longer warn. Swedish, Polish, Dutch and Portuguese
  labels (and, later the same day, Turkish ones) get every allergen and diet warning; before, they raised almost none.
- **Fewer false wheat warnings**: buckwheat ("blé noir", "trigo sarraceno", "grano saraceno", "trigo
  mourisco") and the Italian flours of other grains ("farina di riso", "di mais", "d'avena") no longer flag
  wheat or pay a wheat charge; a bare "farina" still does, since in Italian it is soft-wheat flour.
  Swedish milk ("mjölk", "mjölksyrakultur") no longer conflicts with keto (it read as "mjöl", flour);
  Turkish "süt asidi" (lactic acid), "crème de tartre" and "crema di tartaro" are not milk, "noz-moscada"
  (nutmeg) is not a tree nut, and "kippeneiwit" is egg, not meat. Egg is flagged by its local names too
  (yema, tuorlo, albume, mayonesa, maionese, Majonäse, merengue, Eigelbpulver, Eiklar, Eiernudeln, lisozima).
  German "weiß" is no longer read as Dutch "wei" (whey): white beans, white sugar, white wine and white
  cabbage flagged milk. The rules' word boundaries now count ß as a letter, Swiss spellings ("Griess",
  "Hühnereiweiss") keep their flags and charges, and "Erdnußbutter" is peanut, not dairy butter.
  Other look-alikes from the samples no longer warn: Polish "masa kakaowa" (cocoa mass) and Spanish "masa
  madre" (sourdough) as corn, tamarind as soy, Polish "ostra papryka" (hot paprika) as shellfish,
  "mieszanka" (a mixture) as meat, "porcjach" (portions) as pork, and the town of Balıkesir as fish.
  **Missed flags found by comparing the same product in two languages:** fish by species was read as clear,
  in English too (trout, hake, plaice, eel, swordfish; cabillaud, merluza, Hering, Kabeljau, Seelachs,
  pstrąg and others in each language), as were French "poissons", sulfite salts written as one word
  (Kaliummetabisulfit, Natriumdisulfit, natriumbisulfiet, metabisolfito) and Dutch "eiwitpoeder" and
  "eigeelpoeder" (egg).
  Crustaceans and molluscs by species are flagged too (scampi, cockles, snails, cuttlefish; vieiras, pulpo,
  seiches, Saint-Jacques, seppie, Jakobsmuscheln, Miesmuscheln, inktvis, lulas, kräftor, ośmiornica, ahtapot…).
  Tree nuts too: pine nuts in each language, Italian "anacardi" (cashews), Portuguese "pistácios",
  German "Paranüsse" and "Pekannüsse", and marzipan, praliné, gianduia and turrón by their local names.
  Milk by named cheeses and dairy words (comté, roquefort, gorgonzola, manchego, Topfen, Schmand,
  Butterschmalz, twarożek, kaşar, labne…), and yuba (soy), tahina (sesame), Swedish "senapsfrön" (mustard),
  "Knollensellerie" (celery) and Spanish "altramuces" (lupin).
  Wheat products that do not say wheat are flagged in each language (breadcrumbs, bread, semolina, noodles,
  malt extract: chapelure, Semmelbrösel, knäckebröd, Graupen, Nudeln, extracto de malta, ekstrakt słodowy…),
  while corn semolina, rice noodles and locust bean gum ("St John's bread") stay clear. German "Malz" (malt)
  no longer flags shellfish (it matched the ASCII spelling of Polish "małże", mussels).
  The vegetarian and vegan warnings read carmine, rennet, animal fat, tallow, collagen, compound gelatine,
  bone broth and sausages in each language (cochenille, présure, caglio, löpe, Tierfett, suif, Kollagen,
  Rindergelatine, rosół, Bratwurst, kiełbasa…); microbial and vegetable rennet do not warn, and neither
  does pregelatinised starch, which "gelatin" used to match.
  The vegan warning reads royal jelly, beeswax, shellac, lanolin, L-cysteine, propolis and bee pollen in each
  language ("arı sütü", royal jelly, is no longer milk), and the pork-free one reads Schwein, Schmalz, saindoux,
  tocino, strutto, słonina, suíno, salami and German sausages (not poultry ones, nor Butterschmalz).
  Nightshades are read in more forms (passata, Polish potato starch and paprika, jalapeño, harissa, ajvar…)
  while sweet potato in Dutch, Polish and Turkish, allspice and black pepper ("kara biber") are not; corn
  is read as polenta, grits, nachos, arepa, fubá, zein and HFCS, and gherkins ("cornichons"), garam masala
  and oat grits no longer read as corn.
- **Swedish, Polish and Turkish lists are scored** like their English twins. Before, most were declined, and
  some cleared the gate on a few words and scored far too well (a Swedish biscuit 80 against 25).
  UK "flavourings" now count ([ACCURACY.md](../ACCURACY.md)).
- **Scores**: "FD&C Red No. 40", "Red #40", indigo carmine, Citrus Red 2, spelled-out TBHQ, bromated
  flour, propylparaben and the UK wordings (flavourings, maize starch, skimmed milk, treacle, golden
  syrup, caramelised sugar, fortified flour) now count like their US twins. One corpus label moved: chicken broth, 35 → 33.
- **Speed setting**: *Fastest* and *Faster* now really keep scores 90 and 60 days (cleanup, popup and
  Target badges all follow the setting), and changing it reworks the badges on the page.
- **Report a problem** screenshots only Amazon and Target tabs.
- **Fewer real labels declined as unreadable** in every store language, and real-label scoring fixes
  found by scoring about 650 Open Food Facts lists ([ACCURACY.md](../ACCURACY.md), *Real labels*).
  Processing words (pasteurised, fermented, rennet, yeast, sourdough, smoked, roasted, dried) now
  mark NOVA 3 in every label language, as they already did in English.
  Salt, sugar, vinegar, cream, butter, lard, honey, maple syrup and olive and coconut oil are
  culinary ingredients (NOVA 2) in every label language, as in English ("sucre, sel" was NOVA 3).
  A Polish or Swedish ingredient with the organic word in it ("Kukurydza ekologiczna") was set aside
  as an organic footnote; only the footnote phrasings are now.
  An organic word after the ingredient ("sucre bio", "zucchero di canna biologico", "Biohafer") hid it
  from every rule anchored at the end of the entry, so organic sugar was charged nothing.
  "Non-hydrogenated palm oil" (and "non hydrogénée", "nicht gehärtet"…) was charged as hydrogenated
  fat, 25 where plain palm oil scores 90; hydrolysed protein with its source between the words
  ("protéine de soja hydrolysée") was charged nothing.
  A denial in the list ("sans conservateurs", "ohne Zuckerzusatz", "koruyucu içermez") is dropped in
  every label language, as "no preservatives" is, instead of counting as an unreadable ingredient.
  Peanuts, pistachios, pecans, Brazil and pine nuts, macadamias and the common seeds are whole foods
  in every label language (English "pistachio nuts" and "peanut kernels" too); "possible traces of" is
  a may-contain statement and an origin note ("origine Amérique du Sud") is not an ingredient.
  An allergen bracket that only repeats its parent ("Cacahuètes (arachides)", "yogurt (milk)") is not a
  second ingredient; it still raises the flag.
  "flavouring: vanillin" (and "Aroma: Vanillin", "arôme : vanilline") is charged as vanillin, not also as an
  unspecified flavouring.
  Fruit and dairy sugar, and fruit as a whole food, read every label language: with the naturally
  occurring sugar weight on (the blood-sugar goals), "dattes" and "lait" went uncharged. English "rolled oats",
  "oat flakes" and "brown rice" are whole foods, as "oats" and "rice" are (corpus: rolled oats 95 → 98).
  So are vegetables in every label language, English "tomato" and "potato" in the singular and
  "diced", and prepared nuts ("blanched almonds", "amandes décortiquées", "Walnusskerne").
  Concentrated and clarified butter (Butterreinfett, boterconcentraat, smörolja, burro anidro) earn the
  cooking-fat bonus that "concentrated butter" earns.
  Fat-reduced dairy is read in any word order ("leche en polvo desnatada", "odtłuszczone mleko", Dutch
  "magere_melk_poeder"), and Dutch whole-milk powder waives it like "whole milk powder". "Soaked" earns its
  traditional-preparation bonus in every label language ("trempés", "eingeweicht", "remojadas").
  A second sample of 661 real labels found 27 more words English charges (sucralosio, xarope de glicose,
  sacarosa, sorbitole, kaliumfosfaten, sojaboonolie, koncentrat białek serwatkowych…) and line-break
  hyphens left with their spaces ("mono - i diglicerydy"), which are now closed up.
  Oligofructose and fructo-oligosaccharides are charged as the inulin isolate they are, in English too.
  English "semi-skimmed cow's milk" is charged as fat-reduced dairy (the apostrophe hid it), and everyday
  words from the second sample (coffee, chicory, cultures, rennet, humectants, alginates) are read.
  A hyphen inside a phrase no longer hides it ("partially-hydrogenated" was capped as plain hydrogenated
  oil, "modified corn-starch" and "soya-lecithin" were free), nor does "confectioner's sugar".
  A third sample (715 lists) found accented erythritol ("érythritol", "eritritol"), steviol glycosides and
  iodide in each language, the colon form of a bulking agent ("bulking agent: erythritol", English too),
  "yaourt maigre", Swedish whole-grain durum, and nuts, grains, tea and herbs that read as nothing.
  English "plant oils" and "soya protein isolate" are charged like "vegetable oils" and "soy protein
  isolate"; the mono- and diglycerides rule reads "-glycerider" and "-glicéridos"; and 17 more words.
  A bare code in a function word's bracket ("emulsifier (472e)", "preservative (223)", "colour (150d)")
  is the E-number it names; it was read as nothing. English everyday words from the samples (pectins, pantothenic acid,
  seeds, root vegetables, spices, nigari, caffeine, psyllium) are read, "colouring agent" is an unnamed
  colour, dried and chopped fruit are whole foods, and a pressing claim in brackets is not an ingredient.
  "Stabilising agent", "emulsifying agent", "preserving agent", "anti-oxidant", "acidity correctors" and
  "oil of vegetable origin" are charged like "stabiliser", "emulsifier", "preservative" and "vegetable oil".
  Local gelling agents (gélifiant, Geliermittel, gelificante, geleringsmedel, substancja żelująca,
  jelleştirici, espessante…) and the "agent" wordings ("agent émulsifiant", "agente de conservación")
  are charged like "gelling agent", "emulsifier" and "preservative".
  From the third sample's unread entries: local spices, herbs and vegetables (cannelle, Kreuzkümmel,
  zwarte peper, Möhre, poudre d'oignon…) are whole foods as their English names are; celery, leek, lemon,
  vanilla, chocolate, cloves, mustard, carotenes, anthocyanins, raising-agent carbonates, potassium
  chloride and thiamin are read in each language; "lecithinen", "lesitinler" and "glycérol" are charged
  like lecithin and glycerol.
  Dried egg white in each language (blanc d'œuf en poudre, Eiklarpulver, białka jaj w proszku…) and
  Polish "niacyna" are charged like their English twins.
  A bare "flavour enhancer" (in each language) is an unnamed additive, charged like a ribonucleotide and
  ultra-processed: it scored 80 as NOVA 3 while the same list naming MSG scored 43. A named one is charged
  once, as itself. Dutch "dinatriuminosinaat" is read.
  The word "organic" on its own no longer earns "USDA Organic" (the storefront audit's finding); it is an
  "Organic, unverified" claim worth the same half credit, and a verified certification replaces it.
  **Organic always earns a benefit** (owner's rule, 2026-09-28): on a list where every ingredient is
  penalised (organic cane sugar 81 against 73, organic honey 92 against 85), above a score cap (organic canola
  oil 30 against 25; the cap row says "organic counted above it"), and for meat and eggs whose only organic
  word was in the ingredient list (organic eggs 94 against 86).
  A bare "sweetener" (in each language) is charged like stevia extract, the mildest named sweetener, and is
  ultra-processed; it scored 80 as NOVA 3. It and an unnamed flavour enhancer stand down when the list names
  one anywhere, so "édulcorants, aspartame" (a colon lost to a comma) is charged once.
  The additives that cap the score (artificial colours and sweeteners, industrial syrups, trans fat,
  carrageenan) now also mark the product NOVA 4: "water, sucralose" read NOVA 2 while stevia read 4. No score
  moves (the cap set it already); six sample lists change class.
  Sourcing claims earn their bonus in every label language too (organic / bio, sea salt, sourdough
  and sprouted grain, raw milk, fermented, non-GMO, cold-pressed, butter and olive oil), and an
  organic footnote ("*Organic ingredients", "*issus de l'agriculture biologique") counts as the claim.
  Local flour and starch names (Roggenmehl, Dinkelmehl, épeautre, fécule de pomme de terre,
  Kartoffelstärke, Sojamehl, farine d'avoine…) are charged like their English twins.
  So are flavour compounds (natürliches Vanillearoma, vaniljarom, vanilya aroması, Raucharoma) and
  caramel colour (sockerkulör, Zuckerkulör, caramello semplice, amonyum sülfit karamel).
  Sodium metabisulfite (and UK "metabisulphite") was charged nothing in any language, and PGPR
  (E476) had no rule; both are now charged, with their local names.
  French "amidon modifié" (modified starch), Swedish "äppelsyra" and Portuguese "óleo … alto oleico"
  never matched, because the pattern's word boundary sat against the accented letter; Dutch and
  German hydrogenated fats, compound maltodextrins and guar gums, and "huile d'arachides" likewise.
  Whole milk ahead of skimmed milk waives the fat-reduced dairy charge in every label language, as
  it does in English ("lait entier, lait écrémé", "Vollmilchpulver, Magermilchpulver").
- **Non-food searches stand down in every store language** (toothpaste, shampoo, detergent, pet

## Fixed

| Area | What was wrong |
|---|---|
| Cache | The twice-daily sweep deleted everything after 30 days whatever the speed setting. |
| Amazon requests | Badges and the sorted list had separate queues: up to six requests at once, duplicate fetches, one pausing while the other kept going, and no shared budget. |
| Prices | `€4,99` and `£4.99` were missed, `R$ 4,99` read as 4, a Brazilian unit price was 100× too high, the sort menu was found by English labels, and a digit run could take minutes to parse. |
| Open Food Facts | Two lookups per page view for one barcode. Its allergen markup (`_WHEAT_`, `_SOJA_`) hid the flour charge and the soy warning, because `_` is a word character. |
| May contain | "May contain traces of wheat" (and the same in every label language) was charged as wheat. It now raises the allergen warning and nothing else, to the end of its sentence ("may contain milk, soy, wheat" charged soy and wheat) and brackets included ("cereals containing gluten (spelt, barley, oats)"). A statement of 80 characters or more lost its allergen warning, and its eggs and milk made a bread an animal product with a sourcing charge. |
| Parsing | An ingredient declared with its share ("sugar 20%", "Zucker 45 %", the EU norm) escaped every rule anchored at the end of the entry, a decimal comma ("azúcar 8,5%") split the entry in two, and the EU colon form ("emulsifier: lecithins") hid the additive from its rule.. Brackets inside brackets closed at the first ")": the rest of a sub-list ("…lecithins (soy), vanillin)") stayed in the parent entry, and an unclosed bracket left the rest of the list as one unreadable entry.. A function word in a sub-list ("chocolate (sugar, cocoa butter, emulsifier)") went uncharged because its siblings were recognised, and a bracketed claim ("soybeans (non-GMO)") lost its bonus. |
| Extraction | A placeholder phrase inside a real list ("…; may vary") refused the whole list. Label-photo and barcode reads dropped the page's nutrition numbers. |
| Nutrition | US "Sodium 1,200mg" read as 1.2 mg; an EU "1.580 kJ / 377 kcal" line read as 1.58 calories; EU salt was ignored, so the sodium dial never applied; rows on a "per 100 g" panel were labelled per serving; Dutch, Swedish, Polish, Portuguese and Turkish panels were not read at all. |
| Target | Sodium "1,200mg" read as 1 mg; search badges ignored the speed setting and repainted every 400 ms while the tab was open. |
| OCR | The offscreen host accepted requests from any extension context, skipping the worker's checks. |
| Settings | Import could not be retried after a failure, read files of any size, and export could cancel its own download. |
| Accessibility | Unlabelled avoid list; badges read as bare numbers and pending ones announced per tile; tabs without keyboard support; setup focus did not move; toolbar badge text at 2.15:1; dark-mode text as low as 1.99:1. |
| Permissions | `identity` requested while sign-in is off. |
