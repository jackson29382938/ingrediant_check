# Ingredient Check 1.18.1 — complete readings, words that hid, and a new icon

This release brings together three pieces of work that were waiting after 1.18.0: a review of the scoring path (complete readings, sourcing, nutrition), an audit of how an ingredient can hide from the rules (words broken at a line end, lists written one to a line) together with fixes to the service worker, the page reader and the shared-database script, and a new extension icon.

**It is a patch by number, but it changes food scores.** Five of the 61 products in the calibration corpus move (below). `RULES_REVISION` is `2026-10-28.1`, so every saved score is retired once, and a product reading saved by an older version is read again the next time a page needs it. Personal-care and supplement indices from 1.18.0 do not change: 29 real label formats (antiperspirant, deodorant, toothpaste, mouthwash, shampoo, conditioner, soap, moisturizer, supplement) score identically in both styles before and after.

## Scoring and evidence

- **Nested additive lists keep their family.** `chocolate (sugar, cocoa butter, emulsifiers (322, 471))` now scores the same as `E322, E471` (31, NOVA 4), not 56 and NOVA 3. Compact `322,471` is read in additive-code context; decimal shares stay decimals.
- **Incomplete readings are declined.** Long ingredient entries stay in the assessment, and a long or depth-limited reading is marked incomplete, so a disappearing oil entry can no longer produce a score that looks complete. The shared readability gate declines these on every surface.
- **A word broken across two lines by a hyphen is charged as the word.** A narrow label breaks "Partially Hydro-/genated Soybean Oil", and a photographed label comes back from OCR with that break. It read as "hydro- genated", which no rule matches: on a four-ingredient list that missed the trans-fat cap (63 instead of 5) and let carrageenan, sodium benzoate, MSG and aspartame through at the price of no additive. Each such hyphen is now tried as one word and as two, and a reading is taken only when the rules then charge the entry more. Hanging prefixes ("mono- and diglycerides") and real compounds read as before.
- **A list written one ingredient to a line is a list.** A list pasted from a notes app, or markup separated with `<br>`, has no commas and was one long entry: sugar last in a seven-item list scored 82 instead of 62. Three or more short lines with no comma anywhere are now a list; two lines stay a wrapped phrase. The paste box agrees.
- **Sourcing follows the leading whole food** rather than a four-entry cutoff, so a fifth seasoning no longer removes the sourcing deduction. Organic secondary ingredients cannot imply organic sourcing of the leading animal food, and a sourcing claim is not paid twice.
- **Nutrition is read on one basis.** Declared 100 g and 100 ml quantities use the same comparison; an unknown serving mass leaves the quantity factors unassessed instead of guessing; contradictory compositions cannot earn nutrition rewards. Nutrition extraction stops at nutrient boundaries (an absent total sugar cannot borrow the protein amount), and the barcode fallback converts sodium units and never mixes bases.
- **Organic claims cannot establish unknown ingredients**, and cold-pressed qualifiers soften only their own oil entry.
- **The panel shows what was and was not assessed.** Enabled-but-unavailable nutrition factors are listed ("a missing amount does not mean zero"), and the processing, ingredient, nutrition and sourcing contributions are shown and add up to the score.
- **Saved evidence agrees everywhere.** Certification enrichment saves the exact bounded evidence behind the displayed score, so the product cache, Orders import and history replay give the same number. New snapshots carry an input-schema version; older history comparisons are marked partial without rewriting a recorded rating.

## Speed and reliability

- **No stall on the first score.** The first score on a page compiled the engine's patterns while the reader waited (177 ms for a real label); `engine.warmUp()` now does it in idle time.
- **The service worker answers every message.** A handler whose work threw left its sender waiting until Chrome stopped the worker. Every asynchronous reply now goes through one helper, which replies and leaves a trace in "Report a problem".
- **The housekeeping sweep no longer reads your purchase history** (up to about 12 MB) at every start and every twelve hours, and **order import costs what it changes**: it compares only the records that arrived, not the whole history three times per message.
- **One page reader** for cart lines, order lines, retailer previews, search tiles and the sort's next pages: a streaming size cap, abort handling and one list of the phrases stores print on a block page. An import used to stop for any product with "robot" in its title, and the cart did not know Amazon's own "Robot Check" page.
- A delayed settings read can no longer overwrite a newer OCR checkbox change.
- UK and Ireland unit prices: a pint parsed as 16 oz instead of 19.2 for a `co.uk` store.

## The shared-database script (redeploy `docs/apps-script.gs`)

The script is deployed by hand, so none of this takes effect until you redeploy it; older extensions keep working against the new one.

- Google requests are authenticated before the global sheet lock is taken, and the request log records lock wait and hold times (no tokens, accounts or product content).
- A report or contribution must name a product (a well-formed ASIN or barcode, or a product name or brand); `{}` and unknown request types were written as contribution rows. Every text cell is cut at 10,000 characters.
- The duplicate check for diagnostics batches reads the newest 20,000 rows, not every row ever written.
- A Google token that checked out is remembered for at most a minute under a hash of it, so a settings save or history sync no longer costs one call to Google per request.
- The private history schema keeps certification evidence, and the embedded copy of `purchase-history.js` is re-synced.

## Look

The extension icon is now the leaf-and-checkmark puzzle piece (also used as the README logo). The website favicon is unchanged.

## Also

- The German catalog is translated: 259 messages that were still English, and preflight now fails when a German message is the English text unless it is listed with a reason.
- `npm test` runs in parallel (about half the time) with the timing-sensitive files run alone afterwards; `npm run test:serial` runs everything one at a time. New tests tie the five lists of Amazon storefronts together and pin the wrapped-word and one-per-line readings at every break position. `RULES_HASH` now covers code only, so editing prose in the rule files no longer retires every saved score. The personal-care harness injects a content-script list in one batch, as the browser does, because injecting a file at a time let a search tile finish reading before `src/stores/cart.js` existed.
