# 1.27.0

This release carries out the audit of 2026-11-19 (`docs/AUDIT-2026-11-19.md`). The parser already read almost every
way a list is *written*; what it did not know was many of the *names* an ingredient is sold under. A sugar, starch,
oil, dye or emulsifier under its second name, its British, Indian or EU spelling, its legal name or its INS number
was charged nothing, and so was a processed food named after a whole food. Each is now charged as its usual name is.
No product in the calibration corpus moved: a list written with the usual names scores exactly what it did.

`node docs/audit-verification-2026-11-19.cjs` prints "fixed" for all 88 of its checks.

## Foods named after a whole food

- **Sweetened and dessert dairy.** "Sweetened condensed milk", "chocolate milk", "ice cream", "frozen yogurt",
  "vanilla yogurt" and "sweetened whipped cream" were read as plain milk and cream: NOVA 1, nothing charged.
  "Sweetened condensed milk, coffee" scored 90 and now scores 63 ("Milk, sugar, coffee" scores 65). Condensed milk
  and dulce de leche are charged as sugar; the other sweetened dairy foods as "Sweetened dairy".
- **Processed meat and cheese.** "Mechanically separated chicken", "chicken nuggets", "beef hot dogs", "processed
  cheese", "cheese food", "cheese product", "American cheese", "imitation cheese" and "imitation shrimp" were NOVA 1
  whole foods. "Mechanically separated chicken, water, salt" scored 82 and now scores 41; "Processed cheese, water"
  94, now 45. Frozen chicken, ground beef, cheddar, cream cheese and Greek yogurt are still whole foods.

## Sugars and sweeteners under another name

- About forty names were charged nothing: muscovado, turbinado, demerara and castor sugar; dextrose monohydrate,
  grape sugar, fruit sugar and fructose syrup; glucose syrup solids, liquid glucose and dried glucose syrup;
  isoglucose (capped at 30, as glucose-fructose syrup is); sugar syrup, invert syrup and cane invert syrup; malt syrup,
  malt extract and rice malt syrup; palm sugar and coconut syrup; white grape and pear juice concentrate; trehalose and
  isomaltulose; corn sweetener (capped, as corn syrup is). "Oats, fructose syrup, salt" scored 90 and now scores 63.
- Jaggery, panela, rapadura, sucanat and piloncillo are cane juice boiled down unrefined. They are charged as coconut
  sugar is, a traditional sweetener, not as refined sugar.
- Sweeteners: "acesulphame K" (the British and Indian spelling), rebaudioside A and M, luo han guo and mogrosides,
  thaumatin (E957), neohesperidin DC (E959), tagatose, and "artificial sweetener" with no name, which is now capped
  as a named artificial sweetener is.
- **Sugar split across names.** A cereal listing cane sugar, muscovado, dextrose monohydrate and fructose syrup scored
  65, better than one honest "Sugar" first (63). It now scores 37, as the same split under the usual names did.
- A bare "syrup" no longer makes an entry count as read. A syrup no rule charges now shows as unread, so the coverage
  line reflects it and optional AI help, which rates only unread entries, can see it.

## Additives under another name

- **Modified starch** by its US legal name, "food starch-modified" (90 → 45), and by its chemical names: acetylated
  distarch adipate and phosphate, hydroxypropyl distarch phosphate, starch sodium octenyl succinate, oxidized starch.
  Dextrin is charged as E1400 already was.
- **Seed oils**: "soya oil", "maize oil", "corn germ oil", "sunflower seed oil", "groundnut oil" and "arachis oil" are
  capped as their US names are (90 → 63).
- **Dyes** written with "dye" ("red dye 40", "red dye #40", "yellow dye 5"): 90 → 18.
- **Phosphates**: sodium acid pyrophosphate, tetrasodium pyrophosphate and sodium hexametaphosphate, as E450 was.
- Cellulose powder and bare cellulose, textured wheat protein, hypromellose, "CMC".
- **Flour** by its kitchen name ("all-purpose", "plain", "bread", "cake", "self-raising") is wheat flour, processed
  rather than refined white flour, as unbleached wheat flour already was. Bleached all-purpose flour is refined.
- Emulsifiers and preservatives no rule named, by name and E-number: sucrose esters (E473/474), polyglycerol esters
  (E475), sorbitan esters (E491–495), dimethylpolysiloxane (E900), shellac and confectioner's glaze (E904),
  ethoxyquin, dimethyl dicarbonate, lauric arginate, methyl and ethyl paraben. Sodium erythorbate, sodium lactate,
  4-hexylresorcinol and buffered vinegar are charged a little, as processing aids: they mark a processed food, so a
  plain "chicken, water, sodium lactate" stays out of NOVA 4.

## Label formats

- **Indian labels.** "INS 102" is read as E102: "Sugar, wheat flour, colour (INS 102)" scored 49 and now scores 18, as
  tartrazine does. "Permitted synthetic food colours" is an artificial colour (54 → 20); "liquid glucose" is glucose
  syrup.
- **An additive's class in brackets.** "E500(ii)" and "322(i)" made an entry of the numeral that nothing reads:
  "Raising agents (500(ii), 503(ii)), sugar" read a third of its list and was declared unreadable. It is now read in full.
- **"Percent" in words.** "Contains 2 percent or less of" and "contains two percent or less of" open the minor-ingredient
  block as "2%" does; the next ingredient was charged nothing (90 → 73, as with "%").
- **Characters.** A minus sign typed for a hyphen ("high−fructose corn syrup": 90 → 30), an em dash with no spaces
  between items ("Oats—sugar—salt": 90 → 65), and a Cyrillic or Greek letter that looks Latin inside a Latin word
  ("сane sugar": 90 → 65). A word wholly in another alphabet is left as it is.

## Two rules that charged the wrong thing

- Plain **sorghum**, a grain gluten-free cereals list, was charged as sorghum syrup. Only the syrup is now (84 → 90).
- **Hydrogenated starch hydrolysate**, a sugar-alcohol syrup, was capped at 63 as a hydrogenated oil. It is now charged
  as a sugar alcohol.

## Allergen and diet warnings

Flags never move a score.

- Read now: soy sauce, teriyaki, shoyu, beer, ale, stout and brewer's yeast (wheat; root beer and ginger ale are not);
  hummus, furikake and dukkah (sesame); white chocolate, dahi, malai and khoya (milk); mayo, hollandaise and custard
  (egg; custard powder is not); imitation crab, crab sticks and caesar dressing (fish); satay sauce (peanut);
  gochujang, doenjang, hoisin and teriyaki (soy); wine, sherry and dried apricots unless unsulphured (sulfites).
- A food the label calls vegan or plant-based ("vegan cheese", "plant-based milk", "vegan mayo") no longer raises milk,
  egg or "Not vegan". A "-free" claim now clears the warning it names: "dairy-free cheese" raises no milk warning,
  "egg-free mayonnaise" no egg warning. It clears nothing else: "egg-free noodles" are still wheat, "lactose-free
  milk" is still milk, and "non-dairy creamer" keeps its milk warning, as US non-dairy creamers may contain caseinate.

## Speed

A long run of words broken at a hyphen ("ab- ab- …", "hydro- genated hydro- genated …") took up to 11 seconds to
score at the 6,000-character limit, on the store page's own thread. An entry longer than one ingredient can be is now
judged near each wrap, and the same input takes about 0.1 seconds. `tests/hostile-input.test.js` holds the shapes.

## Panel and settings

- The panel names the entries it could not read ("Not recognised, so not scored: quince syrup, kokum"), up to eight,
  where it used to give only a share.
- At phone width, the settings page's row of section links fades at its right edge while there are more to scroll to.

## Considered, not changed

- Palm kernel oil, palm olein and fractionated palm kernel oil stay with palm oil among the traditional fats, as the
  rulebase has always placed palm oil. Charging fractionated fats is a scoring decision of its own.
- Glucose syrup is charged as a refined sugar while corn syrup is capped at 30; "glucose syrup solids" now follows
  glucose syrup. Bringing the two syrups together would move many European and US products and is left for its own review.
- Pectin, agar, alginates and konjac stay read and free, as the vocabulary has always kept them.

## For developers

- `tests/name-equivalence.test.js` is new: every alternate name must score exactly as its usual name. Add a row
  when a rule gains a name. `CLAUDE.md` says so.
- `RULES_REVISION` is `2026-11-20.1`, which retires every cached score.
