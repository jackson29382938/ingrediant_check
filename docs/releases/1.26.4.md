# 1.26.4

This release carries out the audit of 2026-11-15 (`docs/AUDIT-2026-11-15.md`). Its main finding was six more ways of
writing a list that hid an ingredient from the rules. A list written the usual way scores exactly what it did: no
product in the calibration corpus moved.

## Ingredients that went unread

- **Two ingredients joined by a word, where a rule reads one of them.** "Roasted peanuts and sugar", the opening of a
  common peanut butter label, was charged nothing for the sugar: a rule that read the peanuts kept the pair whole.
  "Roasted peanuts and sugar, salt" scored 80 and now scores 63; "Wheat flour and sugar" 75, now 52; "Water, citric
  acid and sugar" 74, now 61; "Nonfat milk and dextrose" 96, now 63. The pair is split when splitting exposes a
  charge it hid. A rule that reads the pair and neither half alone ("mono- and diglycerides") still keeps it whole,
  and so does a denial ("no artificial colors or preservatives").
- **A joining word before the last ingredient.** "Milk, cream, and cane sugar" scored 90 and now scores 65. "&",
  "with", "including", "added" and "also" in front of an entry, and the joining words of the other label languages,
  are read the same way. The word is taken off only when the rules then charge or read more, so "E 330" and
  "contains sweeteners" stay as written.
- **A hyphen typed as another character.** "High‑fructose corn syrup" written with a non-breaking hyphen, and
  "glucose–fructose syrup" with an en dash, scored 90 and now score 30, as the keyboard hyphen does. A spaced en dash
  still sets the items of a dashed list apart.
- **A footnote mark after the name.** "Cane sugar1", "cane sugar 1" (a superscript once its tag has gone), "cane
  sugar+", "^", "§", "◊", "∆" and "ª" each scored 90 in a list that scores 65. A digit is dropped only when the rules
  then charge or read more, so "Red 3", "Blue 1", "Vitamin D3" and "Polysorbate 80" keep theirs.
- **Text in angle brackets.** "Oats, <red 40>, salt" scored 94 with every word counted as read; it now scores 18.
  Only real HTML elements, closing tags and tags with attributes are removed as markup.
- **"Less than" written with a sign or a colon.** "Oats, <2% sugar, salt" scored 90 and now scores 68, as "sugar
  (<2%)" does. "Less than 2%: sugar" and "<2% of: sugar" open the minor-ingredient block as "less than 2% of" does.
- **A list headed "Contains:".** "Contains: Milk, Wheat Flour, Soybean Oil, Eggs" was taken for an allergen line and
  went unrated. A heading with nothing beside it and a capped ingredient under it is now read as the list. An
  allergen line published in place of the list ("Contains: Milk, Soy") is still not scored. "Contains: sugar, salt"
  now charges the sugar.

## Allergen warnings

These never move a score.

- **Now flagged.** Wheat: "flour" with no grain named ("enriched flour", "all-purpose flour", "cake flour"), "udon",
  "ramen", "noodles" and "pasta" unless another grain is named, "cracker meal", "atta", "maida". Tree nuts:
  "filberts", "chestnuts", "pignoli". Fish: "flounder", "sole", "bass", "isinglass", "nam pla". Milk: "caseinates",
  "lactoferrin", "natural butter flavor". Shellfish: "langostino", "barnacles", "geoduck", "limpets", and
  "glucosamine" and "chitosan" unless the label calls them vegan, vegetarian or fungal. Sesame: "tehina", "til
  seeds", "sim sim". Soy: "okara", "kinako", "bean curd". Egg: "ovomucoid", "ovotransferrin", "vitellin", "livetin".
  Sulfites: "sulphiting agents".
- **No longer flagged.** "Butter beans", "butter lettuce", "cream of coconut", "cream of rice" (milk); "oyster
  mushrooms", "scallop squash", "scalloped potatoes" (shellfish); and what an ingredient says it is free of:
  "gluten-free oats", "egg-free noodles", "wheat-free tamari" (still soy), "dairy-free chocolate". "Lactose-free
  milk" is still milk.

## The worker and the pages

- **The sorted list no longer restarts the service worker every half minute.** Chrome stops an idle worker after 30
  seconds, and the list page reconnected a second later for as long as it stayed open. The page now leaves the
  connection down and answers when the worker asks whether a list is open. Following a new search works as before.
- **A pause after a rate limit is kept.** The AI help's "retry later" and the shared-products back-off were held in
  memory and lost each time the worker stopped. They are kept for the browser session.
- **An AI request gives up after 25 seconds**, under the 30 Chrome allows a worker to wait for a response.
- **Accessibility.** The report page's previews can be scrolled from the keyboard; links in the empty history
  message are underlined (their colour alone was 2.06:1 in the dark scheme); the popup and the settings search are
  landmarks. axe-core reports no issue on any of the eight pages in either scheme.
- **Scoring a long hyphenated run** took a second for 40,000 characters and now takes under a tenth of one.

## For developers

- The release job downloads no packages. It used to install web-ext, with about 340 unpinned packages behind it,
  while holding permission to write to the repository; the Firefox lint already runs in the read-only test job. The
  packages are hashed as soon as they are built, and a test fails if a job that can write installs anything.
- Tools are installed with `--ignore-scripts`. ESLint moves from 9.39.5, which npm reports as unsupported, to 10.15.0.
- `npm run test:coverage` measures the scoring engine (99% of lines, 93% of branches) and CI fails under 97% and 90%.
- Content scripts match `https://` only; Amazon serves nothing over `http://`. Preflight limits their weight: loading
  all forty takes about 23 ms in Chromium, which did not justify loading parts on demand.
- Superseded audit and validation notes moved to `docs/history/`.
