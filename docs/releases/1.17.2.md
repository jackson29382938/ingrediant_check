# Ingredient Check 1.17.2 — words broken at a line end, lists one to a line, a German that is German

Two ways an ingredient could hide from the scoring rules are closed, the order-history import and the service worker
stop doing work they did not need to, and the German translation is finished. `RULES_REVISION` is `2026-10-25.1`, so
cached scores are retired once. **No corpus product moved out of its band, and no existing fixture changed**
(`tests/corpus.json`, the factor fixtures and the arithmetic tests are untouched): the new readings only ever add a
charge to an entry that carried one the rules could not see.

## Scoring

**A word broken across two lines by a hyphen is charged as the word.** A narrow label breaks long words
("Partially Hydro-/genated Soybean Oil"), and a photographed label comes back from the OCR pass with those breaks
in. The line break became a space and the entry read "hydro- genated", which no rule matches. On a four-ingredient
list that missed the trans-fat cap (63 instead of 5), and it let carrageenan (67 instead of 40), sodium benzoate
(67 instead of 36), MSG (67 instead of 34), artificial flavours and aspartame through at the same price as no
additive. `readable` stayed true, so nothing declined them.

Each such hyphen is now tried as one word ("hydrogenated") and as two ("high fructose"), and a reading is taken only
when the rulebase then charges the entry more (a heavier penalty or a lower cap) or reads an entry it could not.
Hanging prefixes ("mono- and diglycerides", "non- GMO") and real compounds read exactly as before, which
`tests/invariants.test.js` pins. Every break position in nine penalised additives is tested.

**A list with one ingredient per line is a list.** A list pasted from a notes app or a bulleted page, or markup that
separates items with `<br>`, has no commas and was one long entry: sugar last in a seven-item list scored 82 instead
of 62, and a longer list was over the length limit and read as nothing. Three or more short lines (48 characters or
fewer) with no comma or semicolon anywhere are now a list. Two lines stay a wrapped phrase, a comma anywhere means
the lines are wrapping a comma list, and long lines are prose. The paste box agrees: a fourteen-line list used to
leave the Score button disabled.

`RULES_HASH` now covers code only. Whole-line comments, blank lines and trailing spaces are not hashed, so editing
prose in the rule files no longer retires every cached score; a comment trailing code, a rename or a reformat still
counts, so the error that remains is an unneeded bump, never a missed one.

## Speed and robustness

- **First score without a stall.** The first `score()` on a page compiled the engine's patterns while the reader
  waited (177 ms for the first real label). `engine.warmUp()` does that in idle time, once, on product, results and
  retailer pages (13 ms for the first label afterwards).
- **The service worker answers every message.** A handler whose work threw (a storage error, say) answered nobody and
  the page waited until Chrome stopped the worker. Every asynchronous reply now goes through one helper, which replies
  with the handler's "nothing to report" and leaves a line in the console and in "Report a problem"'s recent actions.
- **The housekeeping sweep no longer reads the purchase history.** It read all of `chrome.storage.local` (up to 10,000
  records, about 12 MB) at every start and every twelve hours; on Chrome 130 and later it reads the cache's keys only.
- **Order-history import costs what it changes.** Each `purchaseImport` message re-cleaned the whole history three times
  to learn nothing had changed (about 0.4 s of worker time at 10,000 records). It now compares only the records that
  arrived, and a randomised test checks the writes, replies and refusals against the old comparison. The order page
  also stops copying the history out of storage on every quiet moment of a page it has already imported.
- **One page reader.** Cart lines, order lines, retailer previews, search tiles and the sort's next result pages each
  fetched and judged a page their own way. They share one now: a streaming size cap, abort handling, and one list of
  the phrases stores print on a block page. An import used to stop for any product with "robot" or "captcha" in its
  title, and the cart did not know Amazon's own "Robot Check" page.
- **Unit prices, UK and Ireland.** Parsing a size read only a full host as imperial, while pricing a unit also read the
  store key; given `co.uk` a pint parsed as 16 oz instead of 19.2.

## German

273 of the 535 German messages were the English text: the settings page, the sort panel and its menu entries, the
report page and the product panel's failure messages. 263 are translated (the other ten are names, units and "OK"),
and preflight now fails when a German message equals its English source unless it is on a short, explained list.

## The shared database script (redeploy `docs/apps-script.gs`)

The script is deployed by hand, so none of this takes effect until it is redeployed; older extensions keep working
against the new one.

- A report or contribution must name a product (a well-formed ASIN or barcode, or a product name or brand). `{}` and
  unknown request types were written as contribution rows.
- Every text cell is cut at 10,000 characters.
- The duplicate check for diagnostics batches reads the newest 20,000 rows instead of every row ever written, under the
  script lock.
- A Google token that checked out is remembered for at most a minute under a hash of it, so a settings save or history
  sync no longer costs one call to Google per request.
- The embedded copy of `purchase-history.js` is re-synced (`node scripts/sync-apps-script-schema.mjs`).

## Tooling

`npm test` runs in parallel (about 23 s instead of 60 on four cores) with the two files that assert a millisecond
limit run alone afterwards; ESLint 10 with `no-shadow` and `max-len` on `src/`; the purchase-history files are
reformatted and their longest sentences split (no text changed); `tests/amazon-hosts.test.js` ties the five lists of
Amazon storefronts together.

`tests/invariants.test.js` (wrapped words at every break position, compounds, hanging prefixes, vertical lists in eight
separators, the paste box, the warm-up), `tests/rules-revision.test.js` (what the hash does and does not see),

`tests/receiver.test.js`, `tests/unit-sort.test.js` and `tests/amazon-hosts.test.js`. 1,161 tests; the Chromium
harness passes.
