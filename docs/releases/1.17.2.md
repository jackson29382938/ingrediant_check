# Ingredient Check 1.17.2

This update improves Amazon comparisons and fixes issues found in the October audit. Food scoring rules retain their existing behavior. The new category indices update the rules revision and retire prior cached scores.

## What changed

- Sort non-food Amazon searches by unit price, and recognize same-day grocery departments and linked result titles.
- Open comparisons follow new Amazon searches by default, with a setting to control that behavior.
- Google backup saves recheck the selected account and consent. Local purchase deletion no longer depends on interactive authentication; remote deletion can remain pending until available.
- Improve cached score retention, retailer badges and breakdowns, order warnings, and purchase history. New purchase records include source information, and sync sends changes in bounded batches.
- Refresh the website and installation guide with feature pages and screenshots.

## Installation

Download the Chrome or Firefox ZIP from the assets below. Firefox installs are temporary unless signed by Mozilla. SHA256SUMS contains checksums for both packages.

The optional Google Apps Script backend must be updated separately to retain the new purchase provenance fields; older deployments can discard them and cause repeated sync attempts.

## Personal care and supplements

- Add separate label indices for toothpaste, mouthwash, deodorant, shampoo, conditioner, body wash/soap, moisturizers and supplements.
- Choose Evidence-based, Minimal ingredients or Preference-focused in Settings; disable these categories independently.
- Show the category and style on product pages and breakdowns, category badges on search/cart items, and separate averages in mixed carts. Mixed categories do not rank against each other by score.
- Draw an index in gray in every palette, panel, badge, cart line, popup card and toolbar badge. A label index is not a quality band, so it is not coloured green or red by its number, and a cart of one category is named for it rather than given a band word.
- Preserve Supplement Facts and serving information in local readings (up to 4,000 characters, enough for a thirty-nutrient multivitamin; a longer panel was silently dropped at 2,000). Missing functional ingredients, serving/dose evidence, unreadable lists and unsupported combinations stay unavailable.
- Food score calibration is unchanged: no corpus product moved. Preference-focused personal-care indices explicitly reflect avoid-list matches; food allergy/diet/avoid warnings retain their existing behavior.

### Checked against real labels and titles

The first version was tested on one-line fixtures. Against 87 real listing titles it missed 21 products it should claim and claimed 6 it should not, and against real label formats it mislabelled or declined ordinary products:

- **Titles.** "Tooth Paste", "Mouth Wash", "Moisturizing Lotion", "Healing Cream", "Hand Cream", "Castile Soap", "Vitamin C Gummies", "Magnesium … Tablets" and "Biotin 10000 mcg" are now recognized. Dog shampoo, car-wash shampoo, carpet shampoo, shoe deodorant and dish soap are not claimed. Sun care, serums, dry shampoo and 2-in-1 products stay unsupported; medicated creams keep the food gate's own "over-the-counter medicine" notice.
- **Drug Facts labels.** A US "Active ingredient: … Purpose: … Inactive ingredients: …" block was glued to the first ingredient or, on the antiperspirant tried, dropped, so its aluminum salt was never found. The active ingredient is now read first. A label that gives only the active ingredient is rated by the evidence index (which says what it could not check) and declined by the two styles that need the whole list.
- **INCI lists.** "(and)", "Water (Aqua)" and "Aqua/Water/Eau" are read as the names they are. Ingredient names are recognized by their chemical shape, so a 20-name moisturizer (CeraVe, 30% recognized before) is readable; sentences and gibberish still are not.
- **Function lists.** Sulfate-free, ammonium and glutamate shampoos, "Coco-Glucoside", soap salts and quaternary conditioning agents are recognized, so they are rated instead of declined for having no cleansing or conditioning ingredient.
- **Supplement upper limits.** The evidence index for a supplement was 100 for every complete panel, so 9,000 mg of vitamin C scored the same as 90 mg. Each nutrient whose one printed serving is above its adult Tolerable Upper Intake Level now costs 20 points (three at most), with the amount, the limit and the source in the row. Compound weights ("Magnesium Citrate 1000 mg"), mixed vitamin A sources and non-folic-acid folates are not judged.
- **Cautions.** Methylisothiazolinone, formaldehyde releasers, and (in washes and soaps) triclosan or triclocarban each cost 10 points once, on the evidence-based index only.

### Other fixes

- Choosing a different personal-care style, the avoid list, or the category switch no longer retires every saved food score (it did, sending the search, cart and popup back to the network); a food weight no longer retires saved personal-care indices either. Each kind of saved score is retired by its own settings and by the rules revision.
- A saved personal-care index sharing a product's key was shown as a food rating in order history when the order had no saved label reading. It is now excluded from food purchase history.
- The "not scored" panel told people to turn off "Only rate food & supplements", a setting that no longer has that name. It now names "Skip unsupported merchandise".
- The new panel, cart, popup, sort and badge wording, and the category and style names, are in the language catalogs (English and German) instead of fixed English.
- Supplement Facts are shown with the details instead of above the score, and the serving size accepts "One (1) capsule" and "½ tsp".
