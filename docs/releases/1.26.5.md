# 1.26.5

This release carries out the audit of 2026-11-16 (`docs/AUDIT-2026-11-16.md`): a price per ounce that was wrong for most
multi-packs, five more ways of writing a list that hid an ingredient, a half-translated German interface, and API keys
kept where a store page could read them. No product in the calibration corpus moved, and no personal-care or supplement
rating changed.

## Price per ounce

A listing's own unit price, when Amazon shows one, was always read correctly. When it is worked out from the title, only
"Pack of N", "N pack" and "N pk" were read as a pack, so every other wording priced the whole pack as one item and the
multi-pack sorted as the dearest thing on the page.

- **Packs are read however the title writes them:** "(Case of 12)", "box of 12", "8 x 12 fl oz", "12 fl oz x 8", and each
  store's own wording: amazon.de's "(5er Pack)", "Lot de 6", "Paquete de 5", "Confezione da 6", "Verpakking van 4",
  "embalagem com 6". "6 x 500 g (3 kg)" reads as the 3 kg it totals. Two pack sizes that disagree are declined, as a
  weight beside a count already was.
- **A fraction is a fraction.** "1/2 gallon" was read as two gallons (the "2" alone) and "Half Gallon" as nothing; both are
  64 fl oz now, and "1 1/2 lb" is 24 oz.
- **A full stop groups thousands where a comma is the decimal mark.** "Reis 1.000 g" on amazon.de was one gram and priced
  at 85 dollars an ounce; it is a kilogram.

## Ingredients that went unread

- **An unnamed additive after a joining word.** "Oats, salt, and preservative" scored 90 against 78 without the "and", and
  72 other names ("stabilizer", "color", "emulsifier" and their translations) did the same. The guard that keeps "contains
  sweeteners" a statement was applied after every joining word; it now applies to "contains" alone.
- **A purpose written after the ingredient.** "Peaches, water, sugar added to promote color retention" carried no sugar
  charge (80 against 65), and "Milk, salt, sugar added" scored 90 against 65. "added", "added to …", "for …", "to …" and
  "as …" are taken off when the rules then charge or read more. "Color added" and "vegetable oil for frying" stay as written.
- **A footnote digit after a dye.** "Red 401" (Red 40 with a footnote 1) scored 90 against 18, and "yellow 51" likewise. An
  E-number is never shortened ("E 1422" is a starch, "E 142" a dye), and a personal-care label keeps "Red 33" as it is.
- **"Contains" before a capped ingredient.** "Oats, salt. Contains soybean oil." scored 94 as NOVA 1 and "Oats, contains high
  fructose corn syrup, salt" 94 against 30: the statement was taken for an allergen line and its cap went with it. An
  allergen line names sources (soy, wheat), never a capped ingredient, so these now score as the ingredient lists they are.
  The soy and wheat warnings are kept.
- **A denial written as a sentence.** "Does not contain aspartame" was capped at 60 (96 for "aspartame-free"), and "Contains
  no nuts." and "Does not contain peanuts." each raised the warning they deny.
- **A spaced en dash inside a comma list** ("glucose – fructose syrup", 90 against 30) and **angle brackets round a word
  that starts with an accented letter** ("<émulsifiant>") are read.
- **A claim typed as an entry of its own** ("…, organic, non-gmo, fermented, sprouted") counts as a title claim does: half,
  and labelled unverified. Appended to a list of sugar and palm oil, eight such words raised the score from 57 to 72. A
  claim that qualifies an ingredient ("organic cane sugar"), a footnote ("*Organic") and a note sentence count in full, as
  before.

Allergen warnings are not affected by any of this beyond the two denial cases above, and they never move a score.

## German

The purchase-history page was entirely English, and the settings page showed 62 passages in English in a German browser.
Every page's text now has a key and a German string: 542 new messages, 1,257 in all. That includes the 20 goals, their
descriptions and the 45 weights with their explanations. The reasons in the score panel ("Refined added sugar", "caps score
at 30") come from the rule files and are still English in every language.

A test fails if text on an extension page has no key, and preflight fails if a goal or weight has no message, which it could
not see before because those keys are built at run time.

## Privacy and the service worker

- **The OpenRouter and USDA keys moved out of `chrome.storage.local`**, which every script the extension runs on a store page
  can read, to the extension's own database (IndexedDB), which they cannot. A key saved by an earlier version moves on the
  first start. No content script loads the code that reads a key, and a test checks that. The purchase history and the Google
  name and email stay where they were: the Amazon orders page reads them there, and moving saved purchases is not worth the
  risk of losing them.
- **The content scripts no longer compare the address on a timer.** Four of them did, every half second or second for as long
  as a tab stayed open. They now listen to the browser's navigation event, and keep the timer only for a browser without it.
- **The sorted list's rate-limit and back-off state** is kept across worker restarts (1.26.4), and its page no longer wakes
  the worker.
- **DOMPurify 3.4.18** (from 3.4.16), rebuilt from the verified upstream file. Its list of allowed attributes
  is narrower: `enterkeyhint`, `exportparts`, `inputmode` and `part` are gone.

## Accessibility and the website

Keyboard users can scroll the website's five tables, and every extension page and website page is free of axe-core issues in
both colour schemes.
