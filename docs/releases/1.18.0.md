# Ingredient Check 1.18.0 — personal care and supplements

Ingredient Check now reads the label on toothpaste, mouthwash, deodorant, shampoo, conditioner, body wash and soap, basic moisturizers, and vitamins and supplements, and gives each a **label index** of its own. Food scoring is unchanged: no food rule, goal weight or corpus band moved. `RULES_REVISION` is `2026-10-27.4`, so every saved score is retired once and read again under the current rules.

A label index is not a safety score and not a quality band. It reports what the available label can establish, by a rubric written down in [the complete rubric and source limitations](../PERSONAL-CARE.md). It is drawn in gray in every palette (panel, badges, cart lines, popup, toolbar), never green or red, and compared only within one category and style.

## What's new

- **Eight categories, each with its own index.** Product pages show the category and the style with the calculation; search results and cart lines show the category; sorting and cart averages keep categories apart. A cart of several kinds shows one average per category, and a list that mixes categories is not ranked by index (price and review priorities still work).
- **Three styles, in Settings → What to rate.** *Evidence-based* (the default) looks for a function the category needs, a short list of documented cautions, and for supplements a complete Supplement Facts panel with no serving above an adult upper limit. *Minimal ingredients* prefers shorter lists. *Preference-focused* counts matches against your own avoid list. A separate switch turns the whole feature off. The settings file, Chrome backup and optional Google backup carry the new choices.
- **Supplement Facts and serving size are kept** with the reading and shown with the details, up to 4,000 characters (enough for a thirty-nutrient multivitamin).
- **Missing evidence stays unavailable.** An unreadable list, a supplement without a quantified panel or with a proprietary blend, a label that lists none of the function a category needs, a combination (a 2-in-1, a gift set), and unsupported products (sun care, makeup, serums, dry shampoo, medicated creams) are never given a number. The panel says what was missing.

## How the labels are read

The first build of this feature was checked against short test labels. Real titles and labels read differently, and this release is built on them:

- **Titles.** "Tooth Paste", "Mouth Wash", "Moisturizing Lotion", "Healing Cream", "Castile Soap", "Vitamin C Gummies" and "Biotin 10000 mcg" are recognized. Dog shampoo, car-wash shampoo, carpet shampoo, shoe deodorant and dish soap are not claimed. A test pins more than 90 real listing titles.
- **US Drug Facts labels.** "Active ingredient: … Purpose: … Inactive ingredients: …" (also when flattened from the printed table) is read as the active ingredient followed by the inactive list. Read as food, the active ingredient came back glued to the heading, or was dropped altogether. A label that gives only the active ingredient is rated by the evidence index, which says what it could not check, and declined by the two styles that need the whole list.
- **INCI lists.** "(and)", "Water (Aqua)" and "Aqua/Water/Eau" are read as the names they are. Ingredient names are recognized by their chemical shape, so a 20-name moisturizer list is readable; sentences and gibberish still are not.
- **Cleansing and conditioning agents** in sulfate-free, ammonium and glutamate shampoos, soap salts and quaternary conditioners are recognized.

## What the evidence-based index charges

- A supplement serving above the adult Tolerable Upper Intake Level costs 20 points per nutrient (three at most), with the amount, the limit and the source in the row. Doses within the limit earn nothing extra. Compound weights ("Magnesium Citrate 1000 mg"), mixed vitamin A sources and folates other than folic acid are not judged.
- Fragrance costs 10 points. Methylisothiazolinone, formaldehyde-releasing preservatives and, in washes and soaps, triclosan or triclocarban cost 10 points each, once. A claim in the listing's marketing copy ("dermatologist tested") is not label content and moves nothing.

## Fixes

- Choosing a different personal-care style, the avoid list or the category switch no longer retires every saved food score; a food weight no longer retires saved personal-care indices either.
- A saved personal-care index is no longer shown as a food rating in order history, and food purchase trends do not include it.
- The "not scored" panel named a setting called "Only rate food & supplements", which is now "Skip unsupported merchandise"; it names the setting as the settings page does.
- The new panel, cart, popup, sort and badge wording and the category and style names are in the English and German catalogs.
- The serving size accepts "One (1) capsule" and "½ tsp".
