# Ingredient Check 1.19.0 — deodorants and other care products rated and shown like food

A search page of deodorants showed two problems and a missing piece. Some products were not rated at all and showed a "?" (the two Arm & Hammer Essentials tiles). Every product that was rated read 80 or 70, whatever its label. And hovering a rated badge opened nothing, where hovering a food badge opens the "why" card. This release fixes all three for personal care (deodorant and antiperspirant, toothpaste, mouthwash, shampoo, conditioner, body wash and soap, moisturizer) and shows these products the way food is shown.

`RULES_REVISION` is `2026-10-30.1`, so every saved score is retired once. **Food scores do not change:** the calibration corpus and every food test are untouched. Supplement scoring arithmetic does not change either (see "What did not change").

## Why some deodorants were skipped

Two causes, both in how a label was read, and one in what was done with it:

- **The active ingredient was dropped.** A listing that prints the antiperspirant salt after its own name, `Aluminum Zirconium Tetrachlorohydrex GLY (20%) (Active Ingredient), Cyclopentasiloxane, …`, was cut at the marker: the salt was lost, everything after it was taken for "the actives", and the product read as an antiperspirant with no active ingredient. The short headings `Active:` and `Inactive:` were not read either. All of these now read the same (a test pins six ways of printing one antiperspirant to the same 85).
- **A page that prints Drug Facts has no heading called "Ingredients".** Deodorants, antiperspirants, fluoride toothpaste and dandruff shampoo are over-the-counter drugs, and their pages often print only "Active ingredient" and "Inactive ingredients". No list was found, and the search tile said "?". The page reader now reads a Drug Facts block (a new last tier, used only for a product rated as personal care), and puts the active ingredient in front of an "Ingredients" list that shows only the inactive names.
- **A label that listed no recognized deodorizing ingredient was declined, not rated.** A natural deodorant that relies on scent and absorbent powders is a product people buy; it is now rated, and charged for what its label does not show.

The "?" on a tile also no longer blames Amazon for a product the engine recognized but declined to rate: its tooltip gives the engine's reason.

## Scores that differ

The evidence-based index used to be 50, plus 30 for one function, minus 10 for fragrance and 10 for each caution, so almost every label was 80 or 70. It now starts at 100 and is charged for what the label shows, as a food is (the full table is in [PERSONAL-CARE.md](../PERSONAL-CARE.md)):

- **No function the category needs:** 20 to 30, by category (fluoride is the one function with a documented health effect, so a toothpaste without it is charged most). Not a decline.
- **Fragrance:** 15 on a deodorant or moisturizer, 12 on a rinse-off product, because leave-on products are the ones most often implicated in fragrance allergy. Fragrance allergens the EU requires a label to name are charged 2 each (at most 8), and a scent essential oil 4 (never a carrier oil such as coconut or shea, and not in a toothpaste, where peppermint oil is a flavor).
- **Documented cautions:** methylisothiazolinone, formaldehyde releasers and triclosan (washes) were charged 10; they are now 15, with the parabens, phthalates and fragrance chemicals the EU has prohibited. Cocamide DEA and BHA (California Proposition 65) and charcoal in toothpaste (American Dental Association review) are charged 5; propylene glycol and lanolin, which are patch-test allergens, are charged 3 to 4 on leave-on products only. Aluminum antiperspirant salts are the function and are not charged.
- **Only the active ingredient listed:** 25. A label that shows none of the inactive list cannot rule out fragrance or cautions, so it is not scored as if it had. The same antiperspirant scores 85 with both lists, 75 with the active alone and 70 with only the inactive names (15 for the missing active, which the title promises, and 15 for fragrance).
- A score cannot fall below the engine's floor of 3, and when charges would take it there the panel shows the row that says so.

## Shown like food

- **Same bands, words and colors.** An index is drawn in its score's band (Excellent, Good, Mediocre, Poor, Avoid) in the panel, hover card, search badges, cart lines and summary, retailer badges, the popup and the toolbar, in every palette, and takes its verdict from the engine's one table of bands. **This reverses the 1.18.0 rule that an index is always gray.** That rule was made when the index could not tell a clean label from a fragranced one; it can now. What stays different, so the number is not over-read: the caption names the category and style where a food names its NOVA class ("Deodorant · Evidence-based"), the panel carries the note that it does not establish safety, effectiveness or absence of allergens, and a cart of one category is named for it ("Deodorant average · Good").
- **The hover card opens.** A badge such as `85 · Deodorant` never opened the "why" card, because the badge was matched as a bare number. It does now, with the same rows as the product page (What's good, What hurt, Not counted against it).
- **The panel is where a food's is.** A food page has its card under the buy box. A care page has none, and the panel fell through to "Important information", below the product description and A+ content, where it was easy to take for missing. It is now placed above the product overview.
- **A declined index never shows as a score.** An index that declined before reading the list (an unreadable list, a switched-off category) came back with no score and was mounted as a "scored" panel and as a `null` badge. It is now a decline everywhere.

## What did not change

- Food scoring, food weights, the allergen, diet and avoid-list flags, which never move a food score, and the corpus bands.
- The supplement index: still 40 + 40 + 20 less 20 for each serving above an adult upper limit. Its panel and badges now use the same bands, so a complete panel with no serving over a limit reads **100 Excellent**. That says the panel is complete and within the limits, not that the supplement is good; the panel's note says so, and this is the first thing to revisit if it reads as too generous.
- Classification of titles: 12 more real deodorant titles are pinned as recognized; none was missed, so no title was added to the rules.

## Verification

`npm test`, ESLint, `npm run preflight` and the Chromium harness, which now loads a deodorant product page laid out like Amazon's beauty pages and a search page of three deodorants, and checks the panel's position, the three different numbers, the band colors and the hover card. Not run: the live Amazon pages themselves, which this environment cannot reach, so the position of the panel on a real beauty page rests on the element ids Amazon uses (`#productOverview_feature_div`, `#featurebullets_feature_div`) and on the saved-page fixtures.
