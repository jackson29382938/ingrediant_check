Everything Ingredient Check does

A score on every grocery label you look at, the working behind it, and the tools to sort, compare and track what you buy. Scoring always runs on your computer.

Scores on the product page

Open a food product and a panel appears under the title with a score from 0 to 100, a verdict word (Excellent, Good, Mediocre, Poor or Avoid) and the processing level. The toolbar icon shows the same number for the tab you are on.

Click the panel to open the working. Rows are grouped as What's good, What hurt and Score limits, each with its points, so you can see which ingredient moved the number and by how much.

  • Copy score copies the result to your clipboard.
  • Looks wrong? lets you flag a reading you think is mistaken, straight from the panel.
  • Personal care and supplements use separate category-specific label indices. Choose Evidence-based, Minimal ingredients or Preference-focused in Settings. They are shown like food scores, with the category and style named. Unsupported products and labels that cannot be read remain unavailable.
  • An unreadable list is declined instead of guessed. If the rules recognise too little of it, the panel says so and offers a paste box where you can type or paste the label.

Scores on search results

On a supported search page every food result gets a small score badge. Badges come from three places, in order: a score already saved on your computer, the shared certification data, and, when Rate search results and cart items is on, the product's own public page.

  • How rating works. For a result it cannot already answer, the extension requests that product's page from the store you are on, a few at a time and at most 60 per results page. On Amazon the request uses your sign-in, so Amazon sees it much as if you had opened the product. A notice on the first results page says this and has a Turn off automatic rating button.
  • Hover a badge to see why it scored that way. This is worked out locally from what was already read.
  • Click a badge to open the product.
  • Missing labels stay missing. A listing with no readable ingredient list shows a question mark or "Label unavailable", never a made-up number.
  • Speed settings (Settings → Speed) run from Fastest to Most up to date: how long a saved score is reused (90 days down to 1 day), and how many product pages may be requested per results page (20 up to 60). Balanced is the default.

Carts

The Amazon cart shows a score on each item and a cart average under Shopping Cart and on the toolbar icon. It also rates the item on the page Amazon opens right after you add it to the cart.

  • Only your active cart lines are rated. Saved-for-later items, recommendations and carousels are left alone.
  • Quantities, prices, totals, delivery details and checkout controls are never read or changed.
  • Missing public labels can be fetched without your cookies or a referrer. Items with no label stay unscored, and unscored items never count as zero in the average.
  • Optional Earn points for healthy carts (Settings → What to rate) gives points for good items. It is off by default and the total stays on your computer.
  • The retailer previews have cart readers too, checked on saved pages only so far.

Sorting by score or unit price

On a supported Amazon grocery search, Amazon's own Sort by menu gets two more options. Choosing one opens the side panel with that search's listings ranked; Amazon's page is left as it is.

  • Ingredient Check score: High to Low ranks by health score alone.
  • Price per …: Low to High names the unit most listings are priced in: ounce, gallon, count, or 100 g on the metric stores.
  • Price per picker. The panel's Automatic setting follows that majority. Pick any weight (oz, lb, g, 100 g, kg), volume (fl oz, pint, quart, gallon, ml, 100 ml, litre) or count unit instead.
  • Prioritize. Five sliders (health score, unit price, total price, customer rating, number of reviews) from 0 to 10, with one-click starting points: Healthiest, Cheapest per unit, Balanced, Lowest price and Best rated. With more than one factor each listing shows a Match number.
  • Honest conversion. Ounces, pounds and grams convert into each other, as do gallons, quarts and litres. Weight, volume and count never convert across. Listings with no price in your chosen unit go last and are labelled. The picker shows how many listings each family covers.
  • Per serving shows as extra information when the label gives servings per container.
  • Imperial sizes. UK and Irish pints, quarts and gallons use imperial volumes.

Press Alt+Shift+H on a search page to open the list sorted by score without the mouse. Settings → Sort can rate unscored listings while the panel fills and, if you choose, load more results pages (off by default, up to 500 listings).

Sorting is a preview. It runs on amazon.com, .co.uk, .ie, .ca, .com.au, .sg and .in. The unit price is Amazon's own when the results page shows one, otherwise worked out from the price and an unambiguous size or count in the title. It can be switched off in the toolbar popup.

Goals and weights

Two people can read the same label and want different things. Pick the goals that describe you; the score changes to match. There are 20 goals in four groups, and you can combine them.

Everyday

Balanced, General wellbeing, Conventional nutrition, Additive-averse, Whole-food focused.

Body goals

Weight loss / cutting, Muscle gain / bulking, Maintenance, Recovery / rest day, Vitamins & minerals.

Training

Strength training, Running / endurance, High-activity / athlete, Sedentary / low-activity, General fitness.

Diet style

High protein, Low-carb, High-carb / performance, Sugar-conscious, Animal-based.

  • Combining goals. Where two goals set the same dial they are averaged, weighted by how much each goal counts. Settings has a slider per goal, 1 each by default.
  • Plus signs. Some goals turn a minus into a plus: fat-reduced dairy under Weight loss, added vitamins under Vitamins & minerals, honey under Animal-based. Those rows are labelled "a plus for your goals" and capped at +20 in total. A hard ceiling such as an artificial colour never becomes a plus.
  • Every weight is yours. Settings exposes the individual controls, from degree of processing to refined oils, sugars, sodium, fibre, protein and sourcing claims. Imports and slider values are checked and clamped before they are trusted.

Allergen, diet and avoid-list warnings

  • Allergen flags cover the US major allergens plus the EU's celery, mustard and lupin, read in the same languages as ingredient headings.
  • Diet flags mark products that do not fit a diet you chose.
  • An avoid list flags any ingredient you name. Settings suggests common ones to start from.
  • None of these change the score. A warning is a warning, not a deduction. Cross-contact warnings are flagged too.
  • Absence notes such as "gluten free" are not read as ingredients, so they do not trigger a flag for the allergen they name.

Flags are a convenience, not a safety system. Always read the package.

How the ingredient list is found

The best available reading wins, and the panel says where it came from.

  1. The page text. The ingredient and nutrition sections of the product page, in English and in the headings of Spanish, German, French, Italian, Portuguese, Dutch, Swedish, Polish and Turkish pages.
  2. The label photo (optional). When the page has no text list, a photo of the label can be read on your computer with an on-device OCR engine. The pixels are never uploaded. It is turned off for Japanese and Arabic stores, where the English reader misreads labels.
  3. Open Food Facts by barcode (optional). The barcode alone is looked up in the public database, after the browser grants that permission.
  4. You. A paste box on the product page scores whatever you type. It is the best evidence there is, so it is used as written.

Certification data (organic, grass-fed, origin) comes from a small shared table that is requested a few times a day. The request carries nothing about the products you view.

Purchase history and trends opt-in preview

Turn this on in setup or Settings and the extension imports the items on your Amazon.com order pages and scores them with your goals. A dashboard then shows monthly trends from what you actually bought.

  • Order scores show beside the order list, with a page average. Unscored items never count as zero, and non-food purchases are excluded.
  • Scan all Amazon orders walks Amazon's year filters and next links in an ordinary tab, with Chrome left open.
  • History is separate from carts and viewed products: ratings you merely looked at never become purchases.
  • Optional account sync, with Google sign-in, backs history up. It can be synced on demand, exported or deleted, and history is left out of settings backups and imports.
  • It currently covers amazon.com only, and capacity is 10,000 order entries.

Controlled tests cover the import; signed-in live validation was still pending at the last release. Read the full guide.

And the rest

Compare cached scores

When two or more products have saved scores, compare them side by side in the popup, with no new requests.

Light, dark and colour-blind-safe

Match your system, or choose a colour-blind-safe score palette and an accent. Text stays readable at WCAG AA contrast.

Settings level

Choose how many settings to show, from Minimal to Everything. Hidden settings keep their values and keep working.

Back up your settings

Settings back up through your browser's own sync and come back only when you click Restore. Google sign-in is optional.

Report a problem

The popup's report form previews the screenshot, settings and recent actions first. The extension sends nothing; you send the email.

Chrome and Firefox

The same engine runs in both desktop browsers. See install for the differences.
