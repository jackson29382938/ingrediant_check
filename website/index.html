Know what is in the grocery cart.

Ingredient Check for Chrome and Firefox rates grocery labels 0–100 on ingredients, additives and processing. Choose your goals and weights, see what contributed to each score, and keep your scores on your computer.

Install the extension Read the privacy policy

This is a guide to what is written on the label, not health or allergy advice. Always read the package.

See it in action

These are screenshots of the real extension running on sample products. The scores are examples of how the panel reads, not statements about any brand.

Every score shows its working

Open the panel under a product and it lists what helped and what hurt, with the points for each. Nothing is hidden in a single number.

  • Sweetened cereal, 65. Refined added sugar costs 15 points.
  • Chips fried in safflower oil, 63. A refined seed oil caps the score at 63, whatever else is on the label.
  • Plain rolled oats, 98. One whole-grain ingredient, organic, with small bonuses for verified claims.

Sort Amazon by score or by price per anything

Amazon's own Sort by menu gets two new entries. Pick one and a side panel ranks that search, with your priorities as sliders.

  • The menu names the right unit. On a milk search it reads Price per gallon: Low to High; on cereal, Price per ounce; on snack packs, Price per count.
  • Change it in one click. The Price per picker at the top of the panel switches to any weight, volume or count unit, such as ounces, pounds, 100 g, litres, gallons or each.
  • Conversion stays honest. A 32 oz tub and a 2 lb bag compare directly. A gallon of milk does not convert into a weight, so listings with no price in your chosen unit go last and say so.
  • Per serving is shown too, as extra information under each listing.

Example listings shown. Sorting is a preview on Amazon grocery searches; see supported stores.

Pick your goals in one tap

The toolbar popup is the master switch. Choose a goal such as Balanced, Whole-food focused or Additive-averse, and decide whether results and carts are rated. Everything that makes a network request says so next to its switch.

Your weights, your rules

Settings holds the full set: how much each factor counts, allergen warnings, diet flags, an avoid list, sorting, appearance in light or dark, and optional backup. A new install shows the Standard level; Everything reveals every control.

Allergen, diet and avoid-list flags are warnings. They never change a score.

How it works

Score on the product page

On supported food product pages it reads available ingredients and shows a score with a breakdown. Amazon also supports local label-image OCR, permission-based barcode lookup, and pasted labels. Missing or blocked labels stay unavailable.

Scores on search and carts

Supported search results and active carts show ratings from saved labels or available public product pages. Amazon's cart also shows a cart average. Saved-for-later items and recommendations are left alone.

Sort by score or unit price

On supported Amazon grocery searches, Amazon's Sort by menu gets two extra options: score (high to low) and price per ounce, gallon, count or whichever unit most listings use. Change the unit in the side panel.

Your weights, your rules

Choose how much each factor counts, set allergen, diet and avoid-list flags, and see exactly why a product scored the way it did when you hover over its number.

Optional purchase trends

Enable Amazon purchase history to see order-item ratings, averages, charts and the biggest score contributors. You can scan older orders, filter your records, and compare recorded scores with your current goals.

What it covers

  • Amazon storefronts across 21 countries, with the most complete rules for English US, UK, Canada and Australia labels.
  • 15 retailer previews, including Target, Walmart, Costco, Instacart and Kroger. These are still being validated.
  • Local scoring. Scores and product labels are cached on your computer. Automatic rating, local OCR and certification lookups are enabled by default. Settings backup follows your browser profile sync and permissions; other account and sharing features are explained in setup and privacy settings.

Language support varies. See the full store coverage notes.

Install

Ingredient Check is installed from a download, not from a browser store. It takes about two minutes: download the file once, then follow the four steps for your browser.

First, download the file

Open the latest release and scroll to Assets. There are two ZIP files; pick the one for your browser:

Your browserDownload this file
Chrome (116 or newer)ingredient-check-version.zip
Firefox (142 or newer)ingredient-check-version-firefox.zip

Go to the latest release

Chrome

  1. Extract the ZIP into a folder you will keep, such as Documents/ingredient-check. Do not delete or move it afterwards: Chrome reads the extension from that folder.
  2. Open chrome://extensions (paste it into the address bar) and turn on Developer mode, the switch at the top right.
  3. Click Load unpacked and choose the extracted folder, the one that contains manifest.json.
  4. Pin it. Click the puzzle-piece icon in the toolbar and pin Ingredient Check. A short setup opens the first time.

Chrome may show a banner about developer-mode extensions each time it starts. That is expected for this kind of install; dismiss it.

Firefox

  1. Extract the -firefox.zip into a folder.
  2. Open about:debugging#/runtime/this-firefox (paste it into the address bar).
  3. Click Load Temporary Add-on and select the manifest.json inside the extracted folder.
  4. Allow the stores you use. Open the extension's permissions and grant site access for the retailers you shop at.

Firefox removes temporary add-ons when it restarts, so repeat step 3 after a restart. Keeping it permanently needs Mozilla signing; see the browser setup guide.

Check that it worked

  1. Open any grocery product page on a supported store, for example an Amazon food product.
  2. A score panel appears under the title, with a number from 0 to 100.
  3. Click the panel to open it and read the reasons. The toolbar icon shows the same score for the current product.

If the page is not a food product, or the label is missing, the panel says so rather than guessing.

Something not working?

There is no Load unpacked button

Turn on Developer mode on chrome://extensions. The switch is at the top right of the page.

Chrome says the manifest file is missing

You selected the wrong folder. Choose the folder that directly contains manifest.json. Some extractors add one more folder around it; open it and choose the inner one.

I downloaded the wrong ZIP

The ZIP with -firefox in its name is only for Firefox. Chrome needs the one without it.

Nothing appears on the store page

Reload the page after installing. Make sure the extension is switched on in the toolbar popup, and that the store is one of the supported stores. Some stores are previews that are still being validated.

How do I update?

Download the newer ZIP and extract it over the files in the same folder, then press Reload on the extension's card in chrome://extensions. Keeping the same folder keeps your settings and local history.

Google sign-in is optional and local scoring works without it. See the browser setup guide if you want backup across browsers.
