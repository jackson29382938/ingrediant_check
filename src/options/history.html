      Statistical details & error bars

      Confidence intervals use a Student-t approximation with orders as clusters, so three products in one order are not three independent samples. They require at least two rated, identified orders and complete order grouping for item-weighted averages. Purchases are not a random sample; intervals are descriptive, not medical claims, label-accuracy estimates or predictions. Repeated products and household shopping can still be correlated.

        Establish your Amazon history

        Enable import in Purchase settings, then scan all available Orders pages and year filters using your Amazon sign-in. This opens one ordinary Amazon tab; it can work while you use other tabs. Chrome must stay open. Login checks or unsupported pages pause the scan. Imported purchases are kept when you pause or cancel.

        Initial estimate: roughly 15–60 seconds per page of about 10 orders. Cached labels can be much faster; missing labels and photo reading can take several minutes per page. The remaining-time range updates after pages finish and more years are discovered.

        Only confirmed purchases contribute to trends. Quantities, addresses, payment details and raw order numbers are excluded. Up to 10,000 distinct order/product entries can be kept locally. Older account backends need the updated history capacity to sync more than 1,000 entries.
