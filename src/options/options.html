Allergen warnings

Shown as warnings on the panel; they never change the score.

Not a safety check. Warnings come only from the ingredient text this extension could read. If that text is missing, out of date or in a language the rules do not cover, you will see no warning, which looks exactly like no allergen. Never rely on this for an allergy. Read the package.

    Speed and freshness

    Faster keeps saved scores longer and requests fewer pages; fresher re-reads sooner. Balanced is how it has always worked. A product page you open is always read again.

    Saved scores for up to 90 days. At most 20 product pages per results page. Shared readings up to 30 days old.

    Saved scores for up to 60 days. At most 40 product pages per results page. Shared readings up to 30 days old.

    Saved scores for up to 30 days. At most 60 product pages per results page. Shared readings up to 30 days old.

    Saved scores for up to 7 days. At most 60 product pages per results page. Shared readings up to 7 days old.

    Saved scores for 1 day. At most 60 product pages per results page. Never uses shared readings: reads every product page itself, so more requests go to Amazon.

    How fast to request product pages

    Applies to scoring a results page and building the sorted list. Faster finishes sooner but looks more like automated traffic, so Amazon may refuse sooner; five refusals in a row pause everything.

    Two at a time, 0.4 s apart. Slowest, least likely to be refused.

    Three at a time, 0.25 s apart. How it has always run.

    Four at a time, 0.15 s apart. Roughly a third faster than Normal.

    Five at a time, 0.1 s apart. Roughly two-thirds faster than Normal, and the most likely to be refused.

      What leaves your computer

      Scoring is entirely local: the rules, your weights and every score are computed in your browser. There is no analytics and no telemetry.

      Everything that can be sent
      • Shared online database (on by default) — the store and product IDs on a search page, and what was read off products you score, go to the Ingredient Check Apps Script. The spreadsheet behind it is private. Never your settings.
      • Rate search results and cart items (on by default) and Score on hover — request product pages from the Amazon store you are on, using your Amazon sign-in, so Amazon sees them much as it would if you had opened the products yourself. Three at a time, at most 60 per results page, and only for products not already answered. Both are under What to rate.
      • Open Food Facts fallback — sends a barcode to world.openfoodfacts.org when a page has one but no readable ingredients. The barcode and nothing else.
      • Back up my settings (on by default) — Chrome's own sync, for your Chrome account only. Sign in with Google asks Google for your name, email and picture to show who is signed in (kept on this computer only; Sign out deletes them). Saving a copy stores your settings on the Ingredient Check database keyed by a hash of your account id, only when you click it.
      • Send certifications and "Looks wrong?" reports — product identifiers and certification text. Never anything about you.
      • Sort results — with Rate them first, the same product-page requests as Rate search results. Load more results (off by default) also requests further Amazon search pages and product pages, with your Amazon sign-in. See Sort by score or price per ounce.
      • Retailer previews — automatic search rating requests public product pages from the same store, without cookies. Scores stay local; no preview readings go to Google or the shared database. Turn off Rate search results and cart items for saved scores only.
      • Request a store — nothing is sent by the extension. It opens a draft in your email app with the store name and website you typed; you decide whether to send it.
      • Accuracy diagnostics — one row per scored product. This one does record which products were scored; see the section above.

        What a score is, and is not
        
    
        A score reads the ingredient list against a rulebase and your weights. It is a guide to what is written on the label, not a health assessment, and not medical, dietary or allergy advice.
        
    
        Two limits
      
          
        
        • It can only judge what it can read. When too little of a list matches anything the rulebase knows, no score is shown at all rather than a confident guess.
          • 
        
        • Amazon's ingredient text is sometimes incomplete, sometimes out of date, and sometimes for a different size or flavour of the product. The package is the authority.
